Same driving force, different mass

Two systems experience the same net force. One has twice the mass of the other. Predict how their accelerations compare and explain your reasoning.

Solve alone · compare as a group · then check.
Your turnWork it out on paper. Then press Show answer to check.
Answer

The system with twice the mass has half the acceleration, if the net force truly stays the same. Greater inertia means less velocity change per second.

The system includes the hanging mass

Choose the cart plus hanging mass as the moving system. Let M = cart mass + added masses + hanging mass.

The hanger’s weight mₕg drives the coupled motion. The string tension acts internally between the two parts.

mₕg − resistance ≈ Ma

Procedure and safe release
•Measure cart and hanger masses in kg. Secure added masses. Level the track.
•Leave space for the hanger to fall and a soft stop before the cart reaches the pulley.
•Release without pushing. Fit a straight section of v–t data while the hanger is still falling.
•Repeat three times for each of at least three total masses.
•Record all trials and note failed releases separately.
