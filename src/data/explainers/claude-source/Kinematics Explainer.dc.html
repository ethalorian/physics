AP Physics · Lab notebook 00

Before you can explain motion, you have to describe it.

Kinematics is the language physics uses to say where something is, how fast it's moving, and how that's changing — without yet asking why. Every law you'll learn this year is written in that language.

The phenomenon

A car is doing 12 m/s when the light turns red. The driver brakes, slowing steadily at 1.5 m/s².

How far does the car travel before it stops?

You don't need to know the car's mass, the tire friction, or the engine. You only need position, velocity, acceleration, and time. That's the whole point of kinematics. Try it in the simulator ↓

01 · What kinematics is

Four quantities, one variable underneath

Kinematics (from the Greek kinēma, "movement") is the geometry of motion. It builds a chain: position is where you are; velocity is how position changes; acceleration is how velocity changes. Each one is the rate of change of the one before it, and time is the thread running through all of them.

xmeters · m
Position

Where the object is, measured from an origin you choose. Displacement Δx = xf − xi is the change — a vector, not the distance walked.

vm/s
Velocity

Rate of change of position: v = Δx/Δt. Sign tells direction. On an x‑t graph, velocity is the slope.

am/s²
Acceleration

Rate of change of velocity: a = Δv/Δt. Slowing down is still acceleration — just pointing against v. On a v‑t graph, it's the slope.

tseconds · s
Time

The independent variable. Everything else is a function of it. Area under a v‑t graph gives displacement; area under a‑t gives Δv.

CONSTANT‑a EQUATIONS
v = v₀ + at
 
x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²
 
v² = v₀² + 2aΔx
02 · Kinematics vs. dynamics

Describing vs. explaining

Classical mechanics splits into two halves. Kinematics answers how things move. Dynamics answers why — it brings in mass and force. Newton's second law, F = ma, is the bridge: the a on the right side is a kinematic quantity.

Kinematics · how
"Where will it be at t = 3 s?"
  • Variables: x, v, a, t
  • Tools: graphs, slopes, areas, the constant‑a equations
  • Ignores: mass, force, what caused the motion
  • Output: a complete description of the trajectory
Dynamics · why
"What makes it accelerate that way?"
  • Variables: F, m — plus everything from kinematics
  • Tools: free‑body diagrams, Newton's laws
  • Predicts: the acceleration, which kinematics then turns into a path
  • Output: a causal explanation
03 · Why every physics course starts here

Three reasons, one historical and two logical

1
It's how physics itself started.

For 2,000 years, Aristotle's physics tried to explain motion by purpose. Galileo (c. 1600) changed the question: instead of asking why a ball rolls, he timed it down a ramp and found distance grew with time squared. Measuring first, explaining second — that shift is the scientific method, and kinematics is where it happened.

2
You can't write a law about a thing you can't measure.

Newton's laws are statements about acceleration. Momentum is m·v. Kinetic energy is ½mv². Angular velocity, wave speed, orbital period — all are kinematic ideas wearing new clothes. Define x, v, a precisely once, and every later chapter can build on them instead of redefining them.

3
It trains the core skill: reading rates of change.

Slope means rate. Area means accumulation. Vectors have direction and sign. Graphs and equations are the same information in two forms. Those habits are the actual content of a physics course — kinematics is just the simplest place to learn them, because the "stuff" is only motion.
