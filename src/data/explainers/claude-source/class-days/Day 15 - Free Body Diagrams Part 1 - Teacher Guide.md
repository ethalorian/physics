## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Gravity (down) and the normal force from the desk (up), equal in size. Watch for "the book pushes on the desk": that's a force *on the desk*, and rule 6 crosses it out.

**STOP 2 · Box pushed at constant speed.** Four arrows: F_g down, F_N up (equal), push right, friction left (equal, because constant speed means a = 0).

**STOP 3 · Ball at the peak.** **Only gravity.** Common wrong answers: an upward "force of the throw" (it ended when the ball left the hand), or "no forces because v = 0."

**STOP 4 · Car on a curve.** F_g down, F_N up, and **friction pointing toward the centre of the curve**. Many students draw a forward "engine" force or an outward force. At constant speed, any forward drive just balances the small resistive forces, so the film leaves it off; the key arrow is the sideways friction that turns the car. This previews circular motion in Unit 2.

**Person walking** (from your plan, for the whiteboard round if time allows): F_g down, F_N up, and a small forward friction force from the ground on the shoe while pushing off.

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** An **applied force** from the impact, pointing in the direction the spacecraft was moving. It's brief, and it's the one force humans can add.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** F_g down, F_N up, tension along the rope (to the right), friction opposite the motion (to the left). Look for labels and proportional lengths (tension > friction if it speeds up; equal if constant speed).

## Formative checks
- **Whiteboards:** every force included? Arrows proportional?
- **Common errors:** missing normal or friction; "momentum" or "inertia" drawn as forces.
- **Exit ticket:** complete, labeled, proportional.
