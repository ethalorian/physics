## A deliberate choice
Your plan says "plot g vs altitude," while the formative check says the horizontal variable should be **distance from Earth's centre**. The film plots g against r (in Earth radii) and labels each point with its altitude, so students can do both. If your worksheet uses altitude, the curve has the same shape, shifted left by one Earth radius.

## Answers (GM = 3.98 × 10¹⁴ m³/s², R_E = 6.37 × 10⁶ m)
| Altitude | r (m) | g (m/s²) |
|---|---|---|
| surface | 6.37 × 10⁶ | 9.81 |
| 100 km | 6.47 × 10⁶ | 9.51 |
| 1,000 km | 7.37 × 10⁶ | 7.33 |
| 10,000 km | 1.64 × 10⁷ | 1.49 |
| lunar distance | 3.84 × 10⁸ | 0.0027 |

**STOP 1.** N/kg (= m/s²); r from Earth's **centre**.

**STOP 3 · Ever zero?** No: GM/r² gets smaller and smaller but is never zero at any finite distance.

**Reading the graph (no stop).** Half: r = √2 R → about **2,600 km** up. 10%: r = √10 R → about 13,800 km up. 1%: r = 10 R → about 57,000 km up. LEO/ISS (400 km): 8.7 m/s². GEO (35,800 km): 0.22 m/s². Moon's orbit: 0.0027 m/s².

**STOP 4 · Plant the seed.** The ISS *is* falling; it also moves sideways fast enough that Earth's surface curves away as fast as it falls (Newton's cannon). Don't resolve it fully: that's Day 13's argument.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** GM/r² = 4.9 → r = √(3.98 × 10¹⁴ / 4.9) ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶ m → altitude ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶ − 6.37 × 10⁶ ≈ **2,600 km** (your plan's ~2,500 km is fine with rounded values).
