## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Most students choose B (straight out, "flung outward"). The answer is the **tangent** (A or C, depending on the spin direction): with no string there's no inward force, so the ball keeps moving the way it was already going (Newton's 1st Law).

**Direct instruction (on screen).** At constant speed the direction changes, so velocity changes: that's acceleration. The Δv between "before" and "after" velocity points toward the centre. a_c = v²/r; F_c = m v²/r, inward. F_c isn't a new kind of force: always name what supplies it (tension, friction, gravity, normal force).

**STOP 2 · Car.** a_c = 20² ÷ 50 = **8.0 m/s²**; F_c = 1500 × 8.0 = **12,000 N**, supplied by **friction** between tyres and road, toward the centre of the curve.

**STOP 3 · Satellite.** a_c = 7700² ÷ 6.77 × 10⁶ ≈ **8.76 m/s²**. g at 400 km (Day 6) ≈ 8.69 m/s². They match within 1%: tomorrow's orbit argument.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** F_c = m v²/r = 0.2 × 4² ÷ 1 = **3.2 N**, supplied by the **string's tension**.

## Formative checks
- Velocity drawn tangent to the circle; net force drawn inward.
- Students name the interaction (tension, friction, gravity) instead of adding an extra "centripetal force" arrow on a free-body diagram.
