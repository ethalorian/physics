## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** Both **momentum and kinetic energy** are conserved. (An ideal; superballs and billiard balls come close.)

**Setup (on screen).** Σp: m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f. ΣKE: ½m₁v₁ᵢ² + ½m₂v₂ᵢ² = ½m₁v₁f² + ½m₂v₂f². Two unknowns, two equations. When ball 2 starts at rest: v₁f = (m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂) · v₁ᵢ and v₂f = 2m₁/(m₁ + m₂) · v₁ᵢ.

**STOP 2 · Worked.** v₁f = (0.5 − 1.0)/1.5 × 4 = **−1.33 m/s** (bounces back); v₂f = (2 × 0.5)/1.5 × 4 = **+2.67 m/s**. Check: Σp 2.00 → (0.5)(−1.33) + (1.0)(2.67) = 2.00 kg·m/s ✓; ΣKE 4.00 J → 0.44 + 3.56 = 4.00 J ✓.

**STOP 3 · Predict, then STOP 4 · PhET Collision Lab (elasticity 100%).**
- **m₁ = m₂:** ball 1 stops; ball 2 leaves at v₁ᵢ (they swap velocities).
- **m₁ ≫ m₂:** ball 1 barely slows; ball 2 shoots off at nearly **2v₁ᵢ**.
- **m₁ ≪ m₂:** ball 1 bounces back at nearly **−v₁ᵢ**; ball 2 barely moves.

The swap rule is a **1D, equal-mass, elastic** special case: it doesn't cover unequal masses, inelastic collisions, or glancing (2D) hits.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** Equal masses, elastic, one at rest: the superball **stops** (v₁f = 0) and the second ball moves off at **5 m/s east**. Check: Σp 1.0 → 1.0 kg·m/s; ΣKE 2.5 → 2.5 J.

**STOP 6 · Marzano on S3.** Link: a no-contact gravitational flyby is close to elastic.

## Formative checks
- Students check **both** system momentum and kinetic energy for the ideal elastic model.
- The equal-mass "swap" is applied only to its 1D special case.
