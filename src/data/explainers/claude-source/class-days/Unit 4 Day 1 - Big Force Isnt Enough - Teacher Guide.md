## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Rank it.** Most to least: baseball from head height > tennis ball from head height > tennis ball from waist height. Waist → head adds **speed**; tennis → baseball adds **mass**; the baseball from head height has both. For reference (h = 1.0 m and 1.7 m; tennis ball 0.057 kg, baseball 0.145 kg): KE at the floor ≈ 0.56 J, 0.95 J and 2.42 J. Don't show these until after the formula.

**STOP 2 · Save a prediction.** Collect written predictions before continuing. Common answers: "doubles" (linear thinking, often carried over from momentum).

**Reveal (on screen).** K = ½mv², in joules (kg·m²/s²), a scalar. Ratio test: 1 kg at 2 m/s → 2 J; at 4 m/s → 8 J (×4); 2 kg at 2 m/s → 4 J (×2). Then the key distinction: four times the energy is not automatically four times the damage; damage also depends on materials and how the object is stopped.

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** ½(2)(3²) = **9 J**; at 6 m/s: ½(2)(6²) = **36 J** (×4).

**Preview (on screen).** Three payoffs: Day 19 (impact in TNT), Day 20 (escape velocity), Day 18 (heat as the "missing" energy in inelastic collisions). Tunguska (1908) ≈ 10–15 megatons, flattening about 2,000 km² of forest.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** KE changes by a factor of **4** (v² → (2v)² = 4v²). Damage isn't a single quantity: it depends on materials, stopping distance and time, how energy is shared between objects, and what form it ends up in (heat, sound, deformation), so no universal fourfold damage rule follows.
