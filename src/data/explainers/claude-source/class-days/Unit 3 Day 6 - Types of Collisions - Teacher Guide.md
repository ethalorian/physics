## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** Σp before = Σp after, when there's no net external force (negligible external impulse) on the system.

**The three categories (on screen).** Elastic: bounce, no KE lost. Inelastic: bounce, some KE lost. Perfectly inelastic: stick together, most KE lost. **All three conserve momentum**; only elastic conserves kinetic energy. The bars on screen are sketches. Truly elastic collisions between everyday objects are an ideal; superballs and billiard balls come close. Note "perfectly inelastic" loses the *most* KE that's possible while still conserving momentum, which isn't always all of it (the stuck-together carts keep moving).

**STOP 2 · Predict.** Superball keeps the most KE; Velcro carts keep the least. All three keep their system's momentum. Listen for "the clay lost its momentum": its momentum went to the floor/Earth, which is part of the system.

**STOP 3 · Demos (18–32 min).** Run each real demo. The film shows the superball bouncing nearly back up, the clay squashing, and the Velcro carts sticking and moving on together. Where the energy went: heat, sound, and permanent deformation.

**STOP 4 · Pair-share answers.**
1. Car crash, cars crumple: **inelastic** (perfectly inelastic if they lock together)
2. Billiard balls: **nearly elastic**
3. Football tackle, players hold on: **perfectly inelastic**
4. Baseball off a bat: **inelastic**
5. Asteroid impact: **perfectly inelastic**
6. Two magnets repel, no contact: **nearly elastic**
7. A dart sticks in a board: **perfectly inelastic**
8. A tennis ball bounce: **inelastic**

Then the asteroid connection: 2026-XJ striking Earth would be perfectly inelastic (it becomes part of Earth); a no-contact flyby is gravity alone, close to elastic; DART was in between (momentum delivered, spacecraft destroyed).

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** **Perfectly inelastic** (the meteorite and the ground move together afterward). Its kinetic energy became heat (melting and vaporising rock), sound, and the work of digging the crater. Momentum is still conserved: it went to Earth, which barely moves because its mass is enormous.

## Formative checks
- Students separate conserved **system momentum** from **kinetic energy**.
- Sticking together identifies a perfectly inelastic collision; it doesn't make momentum disappear.
