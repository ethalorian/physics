## Scenario values
2026-XJ: **m = 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg, impact speed 8,500 m/s** (same as Day 12; Day 11's practice problem used 10⁹ kg). DART-style spacecraft: 600 kg at 6 km/s → 3.6 × 10⁶ kg·m/s per hit. Substitute your class values if they differ.

## Station 1 · Deflection (STOP 1)
- One hit: Δv = 3.6 × 10⁶ / 3.5 × 10¹¹ ≈ **1.0 × 10⁻⁵ m/s** (0.01 mm/s).
- Target: one Earth radius (6.37 × 10⁶ m). Model: drift = Δv × t.
- **10 years** (3.2 × 10⁸ s): Δv ≈ 0.020 m/s → Δp ≈ 7 × 10⁹ kg·m/s → **≈ 2,000 hits**.
- **6 months** (1.6 × 10⁷ s): Δv ≈ 0.40 m/s → Δp ≈ 1.4 × 10¹¹ kg·m/s → **≈ 39,000 hits**.
- Lead time is the lever (20× more warning, 20× fewer hits). Real ejecta (DART's boost) cuts these by about 3–4×; real orbital mechanics makes an early along-track nudge more effective still.

## Station 2 · Impact (STOP 2)
- p = (3.5 × 10¹¹)(8,500) ≈ **3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg·m/s**.
- F ≈ 3.0 × 10¹⁵ / 0.1 ≈ **3 × 10¹⁶ N** (~10 million Empire State Buildings' weight); depends on the assumed Δt.
- Disasters are compared by **energy**: KE = ½mv² ≈ 1.3 × 10¹⁹ J ≈ **3,000 megatons** (1 Mt = 4.18 × 10¹⁵ J). For scale: Hiroshima ≈ 15 kilotons; the largest bomb ever tested ≈ 50 Mt; the dinosaur-ending impact ≈ 10⁸ Mt.

## STOP 4 · Exit ticket
Any two, justified. Strong pairs: **J = FΔt = Δp** with **small Δv × long time** (deflection), or **conservation of momentum** with **impulse / same Δp, different Δt** (impact).
