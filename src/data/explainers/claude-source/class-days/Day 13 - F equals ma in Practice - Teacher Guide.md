## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** a = 10 N ÷ 5 kg = **2 m/s²**.

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** F_net = 30 − 6 = 24 N; a = 24 ÷ 4 = **6 m/s²**. Watch for students who use 30 N directly (a = 7.5 m/s²): they skipped the net force.

**STOP 3 · Pairs (15–40 min).** Leave paused for your problem set. The card lists the three kinds (find a, find F, net force) and the four-step routine: list forces → net force → F = ma → units and direction.

**STOP 4 · Your turn.** a = (0 − 30) ÷ 5 = −6 m/s²; F = (1500)(−6) = **−9000 N**, i.e. 9000 N backward, opposite the motion. Accept "9000 N backward."

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** **Equal.** Steady 25 m/s means a = 0, so the net force is 0: engine force balances friction. Students often say "engine is bigger, or it would stop": that's the Aristotle idea from Day 9.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** F_net = 12 − 4 = 8 N; a = 8 ÷ 2 = **4 m/s²**. Look for the net-force line before F = ma.

## Formative checks
- **Problem set:** note which pairs stall on net-force problems versus straight substitution.
- **Net force habit:** do students start by listing all the forces?
- **Exit ticket:** net force computed first, units on every number.
