## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Both eggs hit at the same speed, so stopping each needs the same Δp. The pillow stretches the stopping time, so the average force is much smaller. Listen for "the pillow absorbs the momentum" (it doesn't; the egg's Δp is the same) and steer toward Δt.

**Direct instruction (on screen).** J = FΔt = Δp. For a fixed Δp, FΔt is fixed: longer Δt → smaller F. The film shows two force–time pulses with the **same area**: concrete (tiny Δt, huge F) and pillow (long Δt, small F). Examples: airbags, crumple zones, follow-through, padded gym floors.

**STOP 2 · Case study (18–32 min).** Pairs pick one of the six cases; the next card gives a one-line model answer for each. Every answer should say: the same Δp to stop, a longer Δt, so a smaller average force.

**STOP 3 · Sprinter.** Forward = +. Δp = 0 − (80)(9) = **−720 kg·m/s** either way.
- Concrete: F = −720 ÷ 0.05 = **−14,400 N** (about 18 times body weight).
- Foam pit: F = −720 ÷ 0.5 = **−1,440 N**.
- 10× the stopping time → 1/10 the average force.

**STOP 4 · Discuss (asteroid connection).** Short and hard: a kinetic impactor (DART, 2022), a huge force for a fraction of a second; needs accurate targeting and a fast spacecraft. Long and gentle: a gravity tractor or ion-engine push, a tiny force for months or years; needs a lot of warning time. Same impulse, very different missions.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** Rolling back with the punch makes the glove stay in contact longer while the head comes to the same final speed. Same Δp, longer Δt, so a smaller average force on the head.

## Formative checks
- Students hold Δp fixed while comparing stopping times.
- A longer stopping time reduces the average force; it doesn't erase the impulse.

## Next teaching move
Sort exit tickets for "rolling reduces the momentum," "the force is absorbed," or answers that never mention time. Open next class with the two equal-area pulses and ask students to label Δp, Δt and F on each.
