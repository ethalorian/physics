## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Before the hit there's no north–south momentum. The cue ball leaves with some northward momentum, so the blue ball must leave with an **equal southward** amount: it goes **down and to the right**. Tip to tail, the two "after" arrows rebuild the "before" arrow.

**Direct instruction (on screen).** p_x = mv_x, p_y = mv_y. Σp_x before = Σp_x after and Σp_y before = Σp_y after: two separate equations.

**STOP 2 · Worked.** East = +x, north = +y; both balls 0.16 kg. Given (CPA): cue after v_x = 1 cos 30° = 0.87 m/s, v_y = 1 sin 30° = 0.50 m/s.
- x: (0.16)(2.00) = (0.16)(0.87) + (0.16)v₂ₓ → **v₂ₓ = 1.13 m/s**
- y: 0 = (0.16)(0.50) + (0.16)v₂ᵧ → **v₂ᵧ = −0.50 m/s** (south)
- |v₂| = √(1.13² + 0.50²) ≈ **1.24 m/s, at about 24° south of east**

Honors: have students derive the cue ball's components themselves and justify the 24° with tan⁻¹(0.50/1.13). Extension: KE before is 0.32 J, after is about 0.20 J, so this collision is not elastic. (For an elastic glancing hit between equal masses with one at rest, the two balls leave at 90° to each other; here they're about 54° apart.)

**STOP 3 · PhET Collision Lab (2D).** Three problems: predict from Σp_x and Σp_y, then check.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** Momentum is a vector, and the x and y directions are independent: no force acts in one direction because of motion in the other, so each component is conserved on its own. "Total p stayed the same size" isn't enough: it ignores direction, and two different sets of final velocities could give the same total size but different directions.
