## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** Joules (N·m). The object must move, and part of the force must lie along the motion.

**Direct instruction (on screen).** Only the component F cos θ along the displacement does work: W = Fd cos θ. θ = 0° → W = Fd; θ = 90° → W = 0; 90° < θ ≤ 180° → W < 0 (energy taken away).

**STOP 2 · Suitcase.** The 100 N is the **pull**, at 30° above the horizontal; distance 10 m.
- W(pull) = (100)(10) cos 30° = **+866 J**
- W(friction) = (20)(10) cos 180° = **−200 J**
- W_net = **+666 J**

Friction does negative work: in the atmosphere, air does negative work on 2026-XJ, turning its KE into heat and light.

**STOP 3 · Group practice (answers).**
1. 50 N at 40°, 8 m: W = 50 × 8 × cos 40° ≈ **+306 J**
2. Gravity on a 20 N ball falling 3 m (force and motion both down, θ = 0°): **+60 J**
3. Gravity as you lift it 3 m (force down, motion up, θ = 180°): **−60 J**
4. 60 N brake force, bike rolls 5 m forward (θ = 180°): **−300 J**

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** Constant speed means the net force is zero, so your push equals friction: **12 N**. (The 30 N is the box's weight, a distractor: it's vertical, so it does no work here.)
- W(you) = (12)(4) = **+48 J**
- W(friction) = (12)(4) cos 180° = **−48 J**
- W_net = **0 J** (no change in speed, so no change in KE, a preview of the work–energy theorem)

**STOP 5 · Marzano on K1 and R1.**

## Formative checks
- The angle between force and displacement is identified first.
- Positive, negative and zero work are justified from that angle, not from the sign of a coordinate.
