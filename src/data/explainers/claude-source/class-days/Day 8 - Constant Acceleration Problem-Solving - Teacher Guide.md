## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Your turn.** a = (5 − 2) m/s ÷ 2 s = **1.5 m/s²**. Shown at the top of the next card.

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** The card shows each equation with its missing variable struck out. Students invent a problem that needs Equation 3: it should give three of v₀, v, a, Δx and ask for the fourth, with **no time given or asked** (e.g. "how far does a braking car travel?"). The next card names the idea, then walks through the three-step strategy.

**Example 1 (in film).** Car brakes from 30 m/s at −6 m/s²: 0 = 30² + 2(−6)Δx → **Δx = 75 m**.

**Example 2 (in film).** Ball thrown up at 15 m/s: hidden given v = 0 at the top; 0 = 15² + 2(−9.8)Δx → **Δx ≈ 11.5 m**. Ask: "Where did the third given come from?"

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** Bullet train: v₀ = 0, v = 80 m/s, a = 0.50 m/s², t = ?; no Δx → v = v₀ + at → **t = 160 s** (about 2 min 40 s). Shown on the next card.

**STOP 4 · Pairs · problem set.** Your 6–8 problems. Circulate. The prompt asks pairs to write givens, unknown and missing variable before choosing an equation, so you can see their reasoning even when the arithmetic stalls.

**STOP 5 · Share-out.** The chooser table stays on screen: missing t → v² equation; missing Δx → v = v₀ + at; missing v → x equation. Press presenters on *why*.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀), because no time is given and it's the only equation without t. 0 = 12² + 2(−3.0)Δx → **Δx = 24 m** (in the + direction). Assumption: the acceleration stays constant.

## Formative checks
- **Problem set:** which problems are pairs stuck on? Note them for follow-up.
- **Share-out:** are students explaining why they chose an equation, or just plugging in?
- **Exit ticket:** right equation *with* a reason, units on 24 m, and the constant-acceleration assumption stated. A correct number without the reason is incomplete evidence.
