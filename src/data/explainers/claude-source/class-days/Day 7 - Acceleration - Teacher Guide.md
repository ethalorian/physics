## What to listen for at each stop

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Most students say "10 mph per second." That's correct, and the next section confirms it with a 0–60 table before formalizing a = Δv/Δt.

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** Yes, braking is accelerating: the velocity is changing. With forward as +, the acceleration is negative. The Direction section answers this with arrows: speeding up, a points with v; slowing down, a points opposite v. Push on the nuance at the bottom of that card: a negative sign means "points in the − direction," not automatically "slowing down" (an object moving in the − direction and speeding up also has negative a).

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** A speeding up (tilts up), B constant velocity (flat, not zero), C slowing down (tilts down). Watch for students who read B as "stopped": that's a position–time habit. On a v–t graph, flat means constant velocity.

**STOP 4 · Pairs.** Use your own practice graphs. The film models two: +3 m/s² (12 m/s over 4 s) and −4 m/s² (−20 m/s over 5 s). Insist on units and sign.

**STOP 5 · Your turn.** v = v₀ + at = 2 + (3)(4) = 14 m/s. The answer appears at the top of the next card when you continue.

**STOP 6 · Discuss.** Earth's pull grows as 1/r², so it gets much stronger as 2026-XJ approaches; constant velocity stops being a good model when the change in v over the prediction window is no longer small compared with v. Out at 1.2 × 10⁸ km, g ≈ 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ m/s², which changes v by only about 0.4 m/s in 6 months (1.6 × 10⁷ s), out of 8,500 m/s. That's why Day 5's constant-velocity model was reasonable for Earth's pull. The card notes that the Sun's pull is a separate, larger effect handled in Unit 2.

**STOP 7 · Exit ticket.** a = (0 − 30 m/s) / 5 s = **−6 m/s²**. The v–t sketch is a straight line from 30 down to 0 at t = 5 s.

## Numbers used in the film
- 60 mph ≈ 26.8 m/s (rounded to 27); a ≈ 4.5 m/s²; about half of g = 9.8 m/s².
- Car from rest: v = 4.5 × 6 = 27 m/s; x = ½ × 4.5 × 6² = 81 m.
- Earth: GM = 3.99 × 10¹⁴ m³/s²; r = 1.2 × 10¹¹ m; g = GM/r² ≈ 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ m/s².
- 6 months ≈ 1.6 × 10⁷ s → Δv ≈ 0.44 m/s.

## Formative checks
- **Exit ticket sign:** did students get −6 m/s², or +6? A + answer usually means they computed |Δv|/Δt without thinking about direction.
- **v–t interpretation:** can students tell acceleration from the slope, and do they read a flat line as constant velocity (not "stopped")?
- **Equations:** who uses v = v₀ + at confidently at STOP 5, and who needs the reference card in front of them?
