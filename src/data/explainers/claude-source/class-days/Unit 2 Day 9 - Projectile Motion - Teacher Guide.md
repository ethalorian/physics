## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Many students say the dropped ball lands first ("the other one has farther to go") or the pushed one lands first ("it's faster").

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** They land **together**. Gravity acts only vertically, so the sideways push doesn't change the vertical motion; the strobe snapshots stay level the whole way down. The 0 / 5 / 10 m/s comparison is the formative check from your plan: same height → same fall time, regardless of horizontal speed (no-drag model).

**Direct instruction (on screen).** Horizontal: no horizontal force, so v_x is constant and x = v_x t. Vertical: free fall, v_y grows by g each second and y = ½gt². Independent: split, solve each, recombine (Unit 1 vectors).

**Worked example.** 5 m cliff, v_x = 10 m/s: t = √(2 × 5 / 9.81) ≈ **1.01 s**; x = 10 × 1.01 ≈ **10.1 m**. Time comes from the vertical; distance from the horizontal.

**STOP 3 · Pair practice.**
1. 1.2 m table at 3.0 m/s: t = √(2 × 1.2 / 9.81) ≈ **0.49 s**; x ≈ 3.0 × 0.49 ≈ **1.5 m**.
2. 20 m cliff at 15 m/s: t = √(2 × 20 / 9.81) ≈ **2.02 s**; x ≈ 15 × 2.02 ≈ **30 m**.

**STOP 4 · Individual exit.** Look for the independence idea: a named model (horizontal constant v / vertical free fall) and an assumption (no air resistance).
