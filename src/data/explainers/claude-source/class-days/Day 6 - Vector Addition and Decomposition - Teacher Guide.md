## Stops and answers

**STOP 1 · Tip to tail.** The film models A = 3 east + B = 4 north → R ≈ 5 units at 53° north of east (check: √(3² + 4²) = 5). At the stop, students add your provided practice vectors. Watch: arrows proportional? Connected nose to tail?

**STOP 2 · Asteroid resultant.** √(8,210² + 2,200²) ≈ 8,500 m/s, at 15° to the Earth line. That's yesterday's velocity again. Answer appears when you continue.

**STOP 3 · Pairs.** Worked example on screen: 10 m/s at 30° → adjacent 8.66 m/s, opposite 5.0 m/s. Probe: "Why did you use cosine for that component?" (It's next to the angle.)

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** With the angle measured from the vertical, the **vertical** component is adjacent → cos: 10 cos 60° = 5.0 m/s; horizontal → sin: 10 sin 60° = 8.66 m/s. Same answers as before. The recipe follows the angle, not the axis. Answer appears when you continue.

**STOP 5 · Mini-quiz (suggested problems, edit as needed):**
1. *Addition:* 6 m east + 8 m north. → 10 m at 53° north of east.
2. *Decomposition:* 20 m/s at 40° above horizontal. → horizontal 20 cos 40° ≈ 15.3 m/s; vertical 20 sin 40° ≈ 12.9 m/s.
3. *Asteroid:* a different observation gives 8,500 m/s at 20° to the Earth line. → toward Earth 8,500 cos 20° ≈ 7,990 m/s; perpendicular 8,500 sin 20° ≈ 2,910 m/s.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** Look for: the magnitude is the vector's total size (length of the arrow, e.g. 8,500 m/s); the components are the parts along chosen axes (e.g. 8,210 and 2,200 m/s). Components add tip to tail to rebuild the vector; they don't add as plain numbers to the magnitude.

## Formative checks
- **Mini-quiz:** who can decompose correctly? This is the diagnostic skill check.
- **Diagrams:** proportional arrows, connected nose to tail.
- **Formula without understanding:** probe with "Why cosine for that one?"
