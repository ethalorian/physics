## Case study answers
**STOP 1 · FBD.** Solar gravity (toward the Sun, dominant), Earth's gravity (weaker), other planets negligible. No friction, no normal force.

**STOP 2 · F = ma.** Net force ≈ solar gravity, so 2026-XJ accelerates toward the Sun. That bends its path; it doesn't stop it (Newton's 1st Law: it keeps moving).

**STOP 3 · Prediction (Day 5 convention).** Earth at the origin, +x toward the asteroid's starting position. Start (1.2 × 10⁸ km, 0); v_x = −8.21 km/s, v_y = +2.20 km/s; t = 1.6 × 10⁷ s.
- x = 1.2 × 10⁸ − 8.21 × 1.6 × 10⁷ ≈ −1.14 × 10⁷ km
- y = 2.20 × 1.6 × 10⁷ ≈ 3.52 × 10⁷ km
- distance ≈ 3.70 × 10⁷ km
- Window from ±200 m/s: ±0.2 km/s × 1.6 × 10⁷ s ≈ ±3.2 × 10⁶ km per component (the ±50,000 km position uncertainty is small by comparison).
- The real path curves because of the Sun's pull: the constant-velocity model is a first approximation, not an orbit prediction.

## Group problem (STOP 4) · "2026-XK"
Start 1.5 × 10⁸ km; 9,000 m/s at 20° toward Earth; 6 months (1.6 × 10⁷ s). Same convention.
- v toward Earth = 9,000 cos 20° ≈ 8,460 m/s → v_x ≈ −8.46 km/s
- v perpendicular = 9,000 sin 20° ≈ 3,080 m/s → v_y ≈ +3.08 km/s
- x ≈ 1.5 × 10⁸ − 8.46 × 1.6 × 10⁷ ≈ 1.47 × 10⁷ km
- y ≈ 3.08 × 1.6 × 10⁷ ≈ 4.92 × 10⁷ km
- distance ≈ 5.1 × 10⁷ km
- FBD and net force: the same as 2026-XJ (solar gravity dominant).

The name "2026-XK" is just a label for the practice object; rename it if you like.
