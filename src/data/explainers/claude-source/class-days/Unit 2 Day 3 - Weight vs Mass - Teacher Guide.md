## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Discuss.** **Mass** is the same everywhere. Weight changes with the local g. Students often say "I'd weigh nothing on the Moon" or "my mass would be less there."

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** Mass: **2 kg** (unchanged). Weight: 2 × 1.62 = **3.24 N**.

**Worked progression (on screen).** 70 kg student: Earth 70 × 9.81 = 686.7 N · Moon 70 × 1.62 = 113.4 N · Mars 70 × 3.71 = 259.7 N · 2026-XJ surface 70 × 0.0003 ≈ 0.021 N. Mass stays 70 kg; weight scales with g.

**STOP 3 · Worksheet (32–45 min).** Six mixed problems. Insist on kg for mass, N for weight.

**The asteroid question (no stop).** "What would 2026-XJ read on a giant scale on Earth?" Leave it open; it's answered on Day 4 with F = Gm₁m₂/r². (If a student says "just use mg": yes, F = mg ≈ 3.4 × 10¹² N near Earth's surface. Day 4 shows *why* g is 9.81 there.)

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** m = 600 ÷ 9.81 ≈ **61.2 kg**. On g = 25: 61.2 × 25 ≈ **1,530 N**.
