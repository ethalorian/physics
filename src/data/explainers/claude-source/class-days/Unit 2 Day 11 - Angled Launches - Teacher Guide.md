## Answers (g = 9.81 m/s², up = +)
**STOP 1.** At an angle, v_y ≠ 0 at launch: it starts positive (upward).

**Worked: 20 m/s at 30°.** v_x = 20 cos 30° ≈ 17.3 m/s; v_y = 20 sin 30° = 10.0 m/s. Level ground: v_y goes from +10 to −10, so t = 2v_y/g ≈ 2.04 s. Max height = v_y²/2g ≈ 5.1 m. Range = v_x t ≈ 35.3 m.

**STOP 2 · 20 m/s at 60°.** v_x = 10.0 m/s; v_y ≈ 17.3 m/s; t ≈ 3.53 s; range ≈ **35.3 m**, the same as 30°. (Complementary angles give equal range.)

**STOP 3 · PhET table at 20 m/s:**
| angle | 0° | 15° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 75° | 90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| range (m) | 0 | 20.4 | 35.3 | 40.8 | 35.3 | 20.4 | 0 |

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket (physical reasoning).** Range needs both *time in the air* (from v_y) and *sideways speed* (v_x). Steeper angles buy more time but leave less sideways speed; flatter angles have more sideways speed but land quickly. 45° splits the launch speed evenly between the two, giving the best product. Only true for equal launch/landing heights and negligible drag.

## Formative checks
- Initial components labelled correctly (v cos θ horizontal, v sin θ vertical, θ from the horizontal).
- Consistent vertical sign: up +, g = −9.81 m/s² throughout.
- Students who claim "45° always wins": ask about a cliff launch, or a real ball with drag.
