AP Physics · Lab notebook 01

Some questions need a number. Others need an arrow.

Physics keeps two kinds of quantities on purpose. Scalars answer how much. Vectors answer how much, and which way. Neither is "more correct" — the trick is knowing which question you're asking.

The phenomenon

A runner finishes one 400 m lap of the track in 60 s and stops exactly where she started.

Her coach says she averaged 6.7 m/s. Her GPS says her average velocity was zero. Who's right?

Both. The coach asked how hard she worked. The GPS asked where she ended up. Different questions, different kinds of quantity.

01 · Two kinds of quantity

An odometer and a compass

Your car carries both. The odometer only counts up — it doesn't care which way you drove. The GPS arrow only cares which way you're pointed and how fast. Each instrument throws away information the other keeps.

Scalar · a magnitude
"How much?"
distance · speed · time · mass · energy · temperature
  • One number and a unit. 400 m. 6.7 m/s.
  • Adds like ordinary arithmetic: 3 + 4 = 7.
  • Can't be negative when it's a size (distance, speed).
  • Cares about the path, not the endpoints.
Vector · a magnitude and a direction
"How much, and which way?"
displacement · velocity · acceleration · force · momentum
  • An arrow: length is the size, orientation is the direction.
  • Adds tip‑to‑tail: 3 + 4 can be anything from 1 to 7.
  • Sign (or angle) is part of the value: −5 m/s means "5 m/s, the other way."
  • Cares about endpoints, not the path between them.

04 · Why physics is written in vectors

Because directions add, cancel, and split

If scalars are simpler, why does almost every law of mechanics use vectors? Because the world does things scalars can't describe.

1
Effects cancel.

Walk 5 m east, then 5 m west. Distance says 10 m; you're standing where you started. Two forces of 100 N on a rope in a tug‑of‑war can produce zero motion. Only arrows can add up to nothing.

2
The laws point somewhere.

F = ma isn't "a push makes speed." It's "a push this way makes acceleration this way." Gravity pulls down, not "9.8." A car rounding a bend at constant speed is still accelerating — its velocity vector is turning. Speed alone would miss it entirely.

3
Hard problems split into easy ones.

A thrown ball's velocity is one arrow, but it's two independent stories: constant horizontal motion and free fall vertically. Components let you solve each with the one‑dimensional kinematics you already know, then recombine.

05 · When the scalar is the right tool

Direction isn't always information

Vectors carry more, but more isn't free. When the physics genuinely doesn't depend on direction, the scalar is not a shortcut — it's the honest answer, and it makes the math simpler.

K = ½mv²
Energy uses speed

Squaring erases the sign. A ball thrown up at 10 m/s and one thrown down at 10 m/s have identical kinetic energy. Energy bookkeeping is scalar — that's what makes it so powerful.

Wf = −f·d
Friction eats distance

Heat lost to friction depends on how far you slid, not where you ended up. Drive a full lap and the tires still wore down. Path‑dependent costs are scalar.

t = d / v
Fuel, fares, and time

A marathon is 42.2 km whichever way the course winds. Gas burned, fare charged, minutes elapsed — all track distance and speed, never displacement.

|v| ≤ 30 m/s
Limits and thresholds

The speed limit, the sound barrier, escape speed — a threshold on a magnitude. The radar gun doesn't ask which way you were heading.

THE RULE
Reverse the direction. Does the answer change?
NO
Scalar. Distance, speed, energy.
YES
Vector. Displacement, velocity, force.
