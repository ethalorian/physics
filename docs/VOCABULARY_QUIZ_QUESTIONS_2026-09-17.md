# Vocabulary quiz question book

Nine ready-to-review quizzes matched to the published vocabulary sets. Each has 10 questions worth 20 points: four recognition items (1 point each), four interpretation items (2 points each), and two explanations (4 points each).

## Student directions

Choose one answer for questions 1–8. For questions 9–10, use the vocabulary to explain your thinking in a few clear sentences. Meaning and application count. Minor spelling or grammar errors do not lower a conceptually correct answer. Sentence starters are optional.

## Teacher use

In Control Room → Vocabulary → Quiz checkpoints, choose the matching word set with all its words selected, then choose **Load quiz questions**. Review the draft before assigning. These questions do not assign work or create grades by themselves.

Award each written response 0–2 points for meaning plus 0–2 for application using its specific guide below. In each category, 2 means complete and accurate, 1 means partly correct or incomplete, and 0 means absent or incorrect. The quiz percentage remains separate from the teacher’s 1–3 Knowledge rating.

## Vectors & Predicting Position vocab

**Vocabulary:** arrow, combine, component, direction, magnitude, resultant, scalar, vector.

### 1. Recognize · 1 point

Which description defines a vector quantity?

- **A.** It has both magnitude and direction.
- **B.** It has magnitude but no direction.
- **C.** It must always point upward.
- **D.** It must describe a force.

### 2. Recognize · 1 point

Which quantity is a scalar?

- **A.** A displacement of 5 m east
- **B.** A temperature of 20 °C
- **C.** A velocity of 2 m/s north
- **D.** A force of 4 N downward

### 3. Recognize · 1 point

What is a vector component?

- **A.** The sum of all vectors in a diagram
- **B.** The time taken to draw the vector
- **C.** The part of the vector along one chosen axis
- **D.** The number of arrowheads in the drawing

### 4. Recognize · 1 point

What do we call the single vector that has the same effect as combining two or more vectors by addition?

- **A.** Scalar
- **B.** Reference point
- **C.** Component
- **D.** Resultant

### 5. Interpret · 2 points

Two arrows use the same scale. One is twice as long as the other, and both point east. What do they represent?

- **A.** Equal magnitudes in opposite directions
- **B.** Different magnitudes, with no direction information
- **C.** The longer arrow has twice the magnitude; both have the same direction.
- **D.** The longer arrow takes twice as much time.

### 6. Interpret · 2 points

A robot moves 3 m east, then 3 m west. What is the resultant displacement?

- **A.** Zero
- **B.** 6 m east
- **C.** 6 m west
- **D.** 3 m east

### 7. Interpret · 2 points

A displacement has an east component of 4 m and a north component of 3 m. Which description correctly interprets the components?

- **A.** The object must have traveled east first and north second.
- **B.** The total displacement must be 7 m.
- **C.** The north component cancels the east component.
- **D.** The displacement changes position 4 m east and 3 m north, regardless of the route.

### 8. Interpret · 2 points

Two carts each move at 2 m/s, one east and one west. Which comparison is correct?

- **A.** Their velocities are the same because their speeds match.
- **B.** Their scalar speeds match, but their velocity vectors differ.
- **C.** Their speeds have opposite signs.
- **D.** Neither cart has a vector quantity because the speed is constant.

### 9. Explain · 4 points

A map uses 1 cm of arrow length for 2 m of displacement. Describe an arrow representing 6 m west. Explain how your arrow communicates the vector’s magnitude and direction.

*Optional starter:* My arrow would be … long and point … . Its length represents … .

### 10. Explain · 4 points

A rover moves 2 m east, then 3 m north, then 1 m west. Explain how to combine the displacements. State the east and north components of the resultant and describe where one resultant arrow would start and end. You do not need its length.

*Optional starter:* The east component is … and the north component is … . The resultant arrow goes from … to … .

### Teacher answer key and scoring guides

**1. A — It has both magnitude and direction.**

A vector has magnitude and direction. Displacement and velocity are vectors as well as force.

**2. B — A temperature of 20 °C**

Temperature has a value without a spatial direction. The other examples include both magnitude and direction.

**3. C — The part of the vector along one chosen axis**

Components describe a vector along chosen axes, such as its horizontal and vertical parts.

**4. D — Resultant**

The resultant is the vector sum. Addition must account for directions as well as magnitudes.

**5. C — The longer arrow has twice the magnitude; both have the same direction.**

On a scaled vector diagram, arrow length represents magnitude and the arrowhead shows direction. Time is not given by arrow length.

**6. A — Zero**

The two equal, opposite displacements add to zero. The total distance traveled is 6 m, but that is not the resultant displacement.

**7. D — The displacement changes position 4 m east and 3 m north, regardless of the route.**

Components describe the net change along two axes. They do not require a particular route and perpendicular components do not cancel.

**8. B — Their scalar speeds match, but their velocity vectors differ.**

Speed is scalar. Velocity includes direction, so equal speeds with opposite directions give different velocities.

**9. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): A vector has magnitude and direction; scaled arrow length represents magnitude, and the arrowhead indicates direction. Application (0–2): Describes a 3 cm arrow pointing west and connects 3 cm × 2 m/cm with 6 m. The direction must be west, not simply “a long arrow.” For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

**10. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): The resultant is the vector sum; components describe the total change along each axis. Application (0–2): Combines east/west to get 1 m east and keeps 3 m north. The resultant arrow connects the original start to the final position, rather than tracing all three legs. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

## Acceleration & Equations of Motion vocab

**Vocabulary:** acceleration, constant, free fall, gravity, rate, uniform.

### 1. Recognize · 1 point

Which definition describes acceleration?

- **A.** The rate at which velocity changes
- **B.** The total length of a traveled path
- **C.** The location measured from a reference point
- **D.** The time an object remains at rest

### 2. Recognize · 1 point

A report says the cart has constant velocity. What does constant mean here?

- **A.** The cart must be at rest.
- **B.** The velocity does not change over the stated interval.
- **C.** The velocity increases by the same amount every second.
- **D.** The cart covers the same total distance on every possible trip.

### 3. Recognize · 1 point

Which condition defines free fall in the ideal model used here?

- **A.** An object moves downward, whatever forces act.
- **B.** An object has no forces acting on it.
- **C.** Gravity is the only force acting on the object.
- **D.** An object has zero velocity at every instant.

### 4. Recognize · 1 point

A cart gains the same amount of eastward velocity during each equal time interval. Which description fits?

- **A.** Uniform position
- **B.** Constant velocity
- **C.** No acceleration
- **D.** Uniform acceleration

### 5. Interpret · 2 points

A cart’s velocity changes from 2 m/s east to 6 m/s east in 2 seconds. Which statement describes its average acceleration?

- **A.** 2 m/s² east: its eastward velocity changes by an average of 2 m/s each second.
- **B.** 4 m/s² east because the total change is 4 m/s.
- **C.** 6 m/s² east because that is its final velocity.
- **D.** Zero because the direction stays east.

### 6. Interpret · 2 points

A ball is tossed upward after it leaves a hand. Ignore air resistance. While the ball is moving upward, which statement is correct?

- **A.** It is not in free fall until it starts moving down.
- **B.** Gravity stops while the ball rises.
- **C.** Its acceleration points upward because it is moving upward.
- **D.** It is in free fall and its acceleration points downward.

### 7. Interpret · 2 points

A cart moves east at 3 m/s for five seconds without turning. What can you say about its acceleration during that interval?

- **A.** It is 3 m/s² east.
- **B.** It is zero because the velocity is constant.
- **C.** It increases every second because position changes.
- **D.** It must point west because the cart is moving east.

### 8. Interpret · 2 points

A cart’s eastward velocities at successive one-second intervals are 0, 2, 4 and 6 m/s. What pattern is shown?

- **A.** Uniform velocity because the measurements are evenly spaced in time
- **B.** Zero acceleration because the direction does not change
- **C.** Uniform acceleration because velocity increases by 2 m/s every second
- **D.** Increasing acceleration because the cart becomes faster

### 9. Explain · 4 points

Cart A moves east at 4 m/s throughout a test. Cart B moves east at 1, 3 and 5 m/s at successive one-second intervals. Explain which cart has constant velocity and which has uniform nonzero acceleration. Use rate of change in your explanation.

*Optional starter:* Cart A … because … . Cart B’s velocity changes at a rate of … .

### 10. Explain · 4 points

A ball is thrown straight up and then falls back down. Ignore air resistance. At the highest point, a student says, “Its velocity is zero, so gravity and acceleration are zero too.” Use free fall, gravity and acceleration to evaluate this claim.

*Optional starter:* At the highest point, the velocity … . Gravity … , so the acceleration … .

### Teacher answer key and scoring guides

**1. A — The rate at which velocity changes**

Acceleration is change in velocity per unit time. Velocity can change in magnitude, direction or both.

**2. B — The velocity does not change over the stated interval.**

Constant means unchanged. Constant velocity keeps both speed and direction unchanged over the interval.

**3. C — Gravity is the only force acting on the object.**

Free fall means motion under gravity alone, with air resistance neglected. It can include upward motion after release.

**4. D — Uniform acceleration**

Uniform acceleration means the velocity changes by the same vector amount in equal time intervals, so acceleration is constant.

**5. A — 2 m/s² east: its eastward velocity changes by an average of 2 m/s each second.**

Average acceleration is change in velocity divided by time: (6 − 2)/2 = 2 m/s² east.

**6. D — It is in free fall and its acceleration points downward.**

After release, gravity is the only force. The ball accelerates downward while its upward velocity decreases.

**7. B — It is zero because the velocity is constant.**

Constant speed and direction mean constant velocity. Acceleration is zero even though position is changing.

**8. C — Uniform acceleration because velocity increases by 2 m/s every second**

The same velocity change in equal time intervals gives a constant acceleration of 2 m/s² east. Increasing speed does not necessarily mean increasing acceleration.

**9. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): Constant velocity is unchanged velocity; uniform acceleration is a constant rate of change of velocity. Application (0–2): A has constant velocity and zero acceleration. B gains 2 m/s each second, so it has uniform acceleration of 2 m/s² east. Its increasing speed is not constant velocity. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

**10. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): Free fall means gravity is the only force; acceleration describes velocity change and does not have to vanish when instantaneous velocity is zero. Application (0–2): At the highest point the ball momentarily has zero velocity, but gravity still pulls downward and acceleration remains downward. This changes upward velocity into downward velocity. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

## Newton's First Law & Forces vocab

**Vocabulary:** balanced, force, free-body diagram, inertia, net force, push, rest.

### 1. Recognize · 1 point

Which description defines a force?

- **A.** A push or pull on an object
- **B.** The distance traveled by an object
- **C.** A label for how heavy an object looks
- **D.** An object’s resistance to changes in motion

### 2. Recognize · 1 point

What does inertia describe?

- **A.** The force that keeps every moving object going
- **B.** An object’s resistance to a change in its motion
- **C.** The sum of forces on an object
- **D.** The speed gained in one second

### 3. Recognize · 1 point

What belongs on a free-body diagram for one cart?

- **A.** Every path the cart has followed
- **B.** Only the largest force acting on the cart
- **C.** Arrows for the forces acting on the chosen cart
- **D.** All the forces the cart exerts on other objects

### 4. Recognize · 1 point

What is the net force on an object?

- **A.** Its weight alone
- **B.** The size of the largest force
- **C.** Its speed times the elapsed time
- **D.** The vector sum of all forces acting on it

### 5. Interpret · 2 points

A cart has a 5 N force east and a 5 N force west. Its vertical forces also balance. Which conclusion follows?

- **A.** It must be at rest.
- **B.** No forces act on the cart.
- **C.** The forces are balanced and the net force is zero.
- **D.** The net force is 10 N east.

### 6. Interpret · 2 points

A cart starts at rest. The net force stays zero. In the same reference frame, what happens?

- **A.** It remains at rest.
- **B.** It begins moving because zero force means no resistance.
- **C.** It accelerates in whichever direction is forward.
- **D.** It must move at a constant nonzero speed.

### 7. Interpret · 2 points

A puck slides across an ideal frictionless surface after a brief push has ended. Its vertical forces balance. Which description fits?

- **A.** A forward “inertia force” keeps pushing it.
- **B.** It immediately stops when the push ends.
- **C.** It speeds up because there is no friction.
- **D.** It continues at constant velocity with zero net force.

### 8. Interpret · 2 points

One student pushes a cart east with 8 N while another pushes west with 3 N. Its vertical forces balance. What is the net force?

- **A.** 11 N east
- **B.** 5 N east
- **C.** 5 N west
- **D.** Zero because there are two pushes

### 9. Explain · 4 points

A book rests on a table. A cart rolls steadily along a straight track. In both cases the forces are balanced. Explain how both situations can have zero net force even though only the book is at rest.

*Optional starter:* Zero net force means … . The book … , while the cart … .

### 10. Explain · 4 points

A cart moves right after someone stops pushing it. Friction acts left; the track supports the cart and Earth pulls it downward. Describe the forces that belong on the cart’s free-body diagram and explain why “inertia” is not an extra rightward arrow.

*Optional starter:* The force arrows are … . I would not add an inertia arrow because … .

### Teacher answer key and scoring guides

**1. A — A push or pull on an object**

A force is an interaction described as a push or pull. Resistance to a change of motion is inertia.

**2. B — An object’s resistance to a change in its motion**

Inertia is a property of matter, not an extra force that must be added to a free-body diagram.

**3. C — Arrows for the forces acting on the chosen cart**

A free-body diagram isolates one object and shows the external forces acting on that object.

**4. D — The vector sum of all forces acting on it**

Net force combines all forces with their directions. Opposite directions can partially or fully cancel.

**5. C — The forces are balanced and the net force is zero.**

Equal opposite horizontal forces sum to zero. With balanced vertical forces, the whole net force is zero. The cart may be at rest or moving with constant velocity.

**6. A — It remains at rest.**

With zero net force, velocity stays unchanged. If it begins at rest, its velocity remains zero.

**7. D — It continues at constant velocity with zero net force.**

No continuing forward force is needed for constant velocity. Inertia is not a forward force.

**8. B — 5 N east**

The opposite horizontal forces subtract: 8 N east − 3 N west gives 5 N east. Merely having two forces does not mean they balance.

**9. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): Balanced forces give zero net force, which means no change in velocity, not necessarily zero velocity. Application (0–2): The book retains its zero velocity; the rolling cart retains its nonzero constant velocity. Neither accelerates under the stated conditions. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

**10. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): A free-body diagram includes forces acting on one chosen object; inertia is resistance to a change in motion, not a force. Application (0–2): Includes leftward friction, upward support and downward gravity, with no continuing hand force after contact ends. Explains that the cart can move right while slowing because the net horizontal force is left. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

## Newton's Second Law vocab

**Vocabulary:** acceleration, heavy, mass, newton, proportional, proportionality constant, relationship.

### 1. Recognize · 1 point

Which quantity measures an object’s inertia and is measured in kilograms?

- **A.** Mass
- **B.** Acceleration
- **C.** Force
- **D.** Velocity

### 2. Recognize · 1 point

What is a newton?

- **A.** The SI unit of mass
- **B.** The SI unit of force, equivalent to kg·m/s²
- **C.** The SI unit of acceleration
- **D.** A number with no units that describes every motion

### 3. Recognize · 1 point

At fixed mass, acceleration is directly proportional to net force. What relationship does that statement describe?

- **A.** Acceleration stays unchanged when net force changes.
- **B.** Acceleration decreases whenever net force increases.
- **C.** Multiplying net force by a factor multiplies acceleration by the same factor.

### 8. Interpret · 2 points

A student uses kilograms, meters and seconds and writes Fnet = ma. Why is there no extra numerical conversion factor?

- **A.** Force and acceleration are the same quantity.
- **B.** The newton is defined as kg·m/s², so the proportionality constant is 1 in these units.
- **C.** Mass does not affect acceleration.
- **D.** The equation works only for a 1 kg object.

### 9. Explain · 4 points

A team says, “Doubling the net force always doubles acceleration.” Use the relationship among force, mass and acceleration to explain what must stay fixed for that statement to work. Then explain what happens if the mass also doubles.

*Optional starter:* The statement works if … . If both force and mass double, … because … .

### 10. Explain · 4 points

A 2 kg cart experiences a net force of 6 N east. Its acceleration is 3 m/s² east. Explain how the definition of the newton and the proportionality constant in a = k(Fnet/m) make this result consistent.

*Optional starter:* Because 1 N equals … , dividing 6 N by 2 kg gives … . In these units, k is … .

### Teacher answer key and scoring guides

**1. A — Mass**

Mass is measured in kilograms and measures resistance to acceleration. It is distinct from gravitational force (weight).

**2. B — The SI unit of force, equivalent to kg·m/s²**

One newton is one kg·m/s². A net force of 1 N gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

**3. C — Multiplying net force by a factor multiplies acceleration by the same factor.**

Direct proportionality means a constant ratio when the other conditions are fixed. Doubling force at fixed mass doubles acceleration.

**4. D — It is the multiplier that turns the proportional relationship into an equation for the chosen units.**

k is the proportionality constant. In coherent SI units, force is defined so k = 1; changing unit conventions changes the conversion factor, not the physical relationship.

**5. C — The loaded cart has half the acceleration.**

At the same net force, a = Fnet/m: doubling mass halves acceleration. “Heavy” is an everyday description; the quantity compared here is mass.

**6. A — Its eastward acceleration becomes three times as large.**

At fixed mass, acceleration is directly proportional to net force. The law predicts acceleration, not an instantaneous multiplication of velocity.

**7. D — It stays the same because the ratio Fnet/m is unchanged.**

Multiplying numerator and denominator by two leaves Fnet/m unchanged.

**8. B — The newton is defined as kg·m/s², so the proportionality constant is 1 in these units.**

Coherent SI units make k = 1. The absence of an extra factor follows the unit definition and does not remove mass from the law.

**9. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): At fixed mass, acceleration is directly proportional to net force; in SI units a = Fnet/m. Application (0–2): States that mass must stay fixed for doubled force to double acceleration. If force and mass both double, the ratio and acceleration stay unchanged. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

**10. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): A newton is kg·m/s²; the proportionality constant k is 1 when these coherent SI units are used. Application (0–2): Shows or explains that 6 N ÷ 2 kg gives 3 m/s² and that acceleration points with the net force (east). Connects the unit cancellation and k = 1 rather than treating force as velocity. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

*Optional starter:* The book is in equilibrium because … . The third-law partner is … acting on … .

### 10. Explain · 4 points

*Optional starter:* As the surfaces rub, friction … . The normal force instead … .

### Teacher answer key and scoring guides

**1. A — Equal-magnitude, opposite-direction forces that act on two different objects**

A Newton’s third-law pair comes from one interaction: A on B and B on A, equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, acting simultaneously on different objects.

**2. B — A surface’s support force perpendicular to the contact surface**

The normal force is perpendicular to the contact surface. On a tilted surface it is not vertical.

**3. C — Relative sliding or the tendency to slide between the surfaces**

Friction resists relative sliding at the contact. It can help an object move relative to the room, as when a person walks.

**4. D — The forces on it sum to zero.**

Translational equilibrium means zero net force and zero acceleration. The object may be at rest or moving with constant velocity.

**5. B — Perpendicular to the ramp, away from its surface**

“Normal” means perpendicular to the contact surface. It is not a synonym for vertical.

**6. D — Left, opposing the box’s sliding at the contact**

For this sliding box, the floor’s kinetic friction on the box points left, opposite its rightward relative sliding.

**7. A — The wall’s equal-magnitude, opposite-direction force on the hand**

The partner reverses the two interacting objects: hand on wall pairs with wall on hand.

**8. C — They have equal magnitude and opposite direction, giving zero horizontal net force.**

Constant velocity means zero acceleration and net force. Pull and friction balance on the crate, but are not a third-law pair because both act on the same object.

**9. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): Equilibrium concerns forces adding to zero on one object. An action-reaction pair consists of equal opposite forces on two different interacting objects. Application (0–2): Both stated forces act on the book and balance it. The partner to the table-on-book force is the book-on-table force, which acts on the table. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.

**10. Written response**

Meaning (0–2): Friction opposes relative sliding along the contact; the normal force acts perpendicular to the surface. Rubbing describes surfaces moving against one another. Application (0–2): Describes the block sliding relative to the table, friction acting backward along the table and the normal force acting upward from the table. Does not call normal force a second backward friction force. For each category: 2 = complete and accurate, 1 = partly correct or incomplete, 0 = absent or incorrect. Accept equivalent wording; minor spelling or grammar errors do not reduce the score.
