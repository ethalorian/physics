Explanation and application

Units before multiplication

A train travels at 40 m/s for 2 hours. Find its displacement if velocity stays constant.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 55 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Units before multiplication

2 h × 3,600 s/h = 7,200 s.

Δx = (40 m/s)(7,200 s) = 288,000 m = 288 km.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 56 / 24
Explanation and application

Position includes the starting point

xf = x0 + vxt

For constant velocity, displacement is vₓt. The initial position can be nonzero.

In two dimensions, also use yf = y0 + vyt.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 57 / 24
Explanation and application

A local prediction

A cart starts at x₀ = 12 m and moves at −2 m/s for 4 s. Find displacement and final position.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 58 / 24
Solution and reasoning

A local prediction

Δx = (−2)(4) = −8 m.

xf = 12 + (−8) = 4 m.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 511 / 24
Explanation and application

Position and displacement are different vectors

The model travels about 1.36 × 10⁸ km in six months. Why is that different from the final 3.70 × 10⁷ km separation from Earth?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 512 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Position and displacement are different vectors

Travel distance describes the path covered. Final separation measures the endpoint from Earth’s origin. The asteroid began far from that origin and moved diagonally past its x-coordinate.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 513 / 24
Explanation and application

A measurement interval is a bound

A position written 100 ± 2 m means values from 98 to 102 m under the stated bound.

For x = x₀ + vt with t ≥ 0, independent bounded one-dimensional inputs give:

δxf = δx0 + t δv

This is a conservative bound, not automatically a confidence interval.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 514 / 24
Explanation and application

Guided uncertainty

A cart starts at 10 ± 1 m and moves at 2.0 ± 0.2 m/s for exactly 5 s. Find the central prediction, its bound, and the full width.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 515 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Guided uncertainty

Central x = 10 + 2(5) = 20 m.

Half-width = 1 + 5(0.2) = 2 m. Interval 18–22 m, full width 4 m.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 516 / 24
Explanation and application

The asteroid’s simplified bounds

Treat the speed bound as ±0.2 km/s at a fixed 15° angle, with initial x bound ±50,000 km and y₀ exact in this teaching model.

δx ≈ 50,000 + t(0.2 cos 15°) ≈ 3.14 × 10⁶ km
δy ≈ t(0.2 sin 15°) ≈ 8.28 × 10⁵ km

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 518 / 24
Explanation and application

Model error is another uncertainty

Suppose velocity changes because of gravity. Would narrower position and speed measurement bounds make the constant-velocity prediction exact?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 519 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Model error is another uncertainty

No. Better measurements reduce input uncertainty. They do not remove error from an unsuitable constant-velocity model. Gravity and Earth’s motion matter for an actual long-term orbit prediction.

Independent prediction

An object starts at 30 ± 2 m with velocity −3.0 ± 0.1 m/s. Predict x after 10 s and give the full uncertainty interval.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 521 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Independent prediction

Central x = 30 − 30 = 0 m. Half-width = 2 + 10(0.1) = 3 m.

Interval −3 to +3 m, full width 6 m.
