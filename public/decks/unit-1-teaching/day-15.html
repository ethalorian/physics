Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 156 / 21
Explanation and application

A diagram is a force inventory

  • Name the object or system boundary.
  • Represent it with a dot or simple body outline.
  • Draw one arrow for each actual external force, starting at the object.
  • Label the force type and agent. Choose axes.
  • Use relative arrow lengths when magnitudes are known or constrained.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1512 / 21
Explanation and application

Components do not create extra interactions

When resolving a force, replace it with its components in the equations.

A component diagram may use dashed guides. Do not count the original force and both components in the same sum.

For a ramp, weight still points vertically downward before decomposition.

Critiquing an incorrect diagram

A box’s diagram includes weight, normal, friction, a “velocity force,” and an extra net-force arrow. What should change?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1516 / 21
Solution and reasoning

Critiquing an incorrect diagram

Remove the velocity force. Net force is the vector sum of the interaction arrows, so show it separately if needed.

Check that friction has an actual surface source and the direction matches relative slipping or its tendency.

An asteroid’s force inventory

Model an asteroid away from surfaces with no active engine. Name plausible external interactions. Is “forward motion” an interaction?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1518 / 21
Solution and reasoning

An asteroid’s force inventory

Gravitational forces from the Sun, Earth, and other bodies may act. Their relevance depends on the chosen approximation.

Forward motion is a state, not an interaction. No surface contact means no normal force or ordinary surface friction.
