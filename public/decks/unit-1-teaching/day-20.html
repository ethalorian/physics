Find the probe’s final coordinates, displacement magnitude, and separation from the origin.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 207 / 22
Solution and reasoning

Worked case: endpoint and separation

x = −100 + 4(20) = −20 km. y = 0 + 3(20) = 60 km.

Displacement = (80,60) km, magnitude 100 km. Separation from origin = √(20²+60²) ≈ 63.2 km.

Worked case: the bounds

δx = 1 + 20(0.1) = 3 km, so x lies from −23 to −17 km.

δy = 20(0.1) = 2 km, so y lies from 58 to 62 km.

Worked public explanation

“Under our constant-velocity model, the probe should reach about 20 km left and 60 km above the origin after 20 seconds. The measurement bounds give x from −23 to −17 km and y from 58 to 62 km. A new force changes the prediction

A 1,000 kg probe experiences a constant net force of +200 N along x for 10 s. Find the extra Δvₓ and extra displacement relative to a constant-velocity prediction with the same initial state.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 2016 / 22
Solution and reasoning

A new force changes the prediction

aₓ = 200/1,000 = 0.20 m/s².

Extra Δvₓ = 2 m/s. Extra Δx = ½(0.20)(10²) = 10 m.
