A push that does not move the box

Solution and reasoning

A push that does not move the box

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 176 / 21
Explanation and application

Two friction models

0 ≤ fs ≤ μsN
fk ≈ μkN

Static friction adjusts while the contact does not slip. Kinetic friction models sliding contact.

The coefficients depend on the contacting surfaces and conditions.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 177 / 21
Explanation and application

Before and after slipping

A 5 kg box has μs = 0.40 and μk = 0.30. A horizontal 12 N push acts on a level floor. Find N, the maximum static friction, and the actual friction.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 178 / 21
Solution and reasoning

Before and after slipping

N = 49 N. fs,max = 0.40(49) = 19.6 N.

Since 12 N is below that limit, actual fs = 12 N opposite the push. The box stays at rest.

Explanation and application

The same box with a larger push

The push rises to 25 N and the same box begins sliding. Find kinetic friction and acceleration.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1710 / 21
Solution and reasoning

The same box with a larger push

fk = 0.30(49) = 14.7 N.

ΣF = 25 − 14.7 = 10.3 N. a = 10.3/5 = 2.06 m/s².

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1711 / 21
Investigation 1.5

Measuring the two coefficients

  • Measure the sliding block’s mass. Pull horizontally with a zeroed force sensor.
  • Increase the pull slowly until slipping begins. Record the peak just before sliding.
  • Then pull at approximately constant speed and average the steady sliding force.
  • Repeat three times. Compute μs ≈ Fpeak/N and μk ≈ Fsteady/N.
  • Record surfaces, mass, trial spread, and sources of uncertainty.
