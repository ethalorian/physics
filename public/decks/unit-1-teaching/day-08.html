Three equations, one model

EquationUseful when…
v = v₀ + atYou need velocity or time
Δx = v₀t + ½at²You know elapsed time
v² = v₀² + 2aΔxTime is not given or needed

All three assume constant acceleration over the interval. Define the axis before assigning signs.

Worked example

Worked braking distance

A car moves at +20 m/s and brakes at constant −4 m/s² until it stops. How far does it travel?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 89 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Worked braking distance

0² = 20² + 2(−4)Δx
Δx = −400 / (−8) = +50 m

Time is unnecessary. As a check, t = 5 s and average velocity = 10 m/s, giving 50 m.

Guided launch from rest

Vertical motion uses the same tools

A ball launches upward at 14 m/s. Ignore air resistance and use upward as positive, a = −9.8 m/s². Find the rise to its highest point.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 815 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Vertical motion uses the same tools

At the highest point v = 0.

0 = 14² + 2(−9.8)Δy, so Δy = 10 m. Acceleration is still downward at the top.

Error analysis: beyond the stopping point

A car starts at +12 m/s with a = −3 m/s². A student uses the same acceleration for 8 s and concludes v = −12 m/s. What assumption needs checking?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 821 / 24
Solution and reasoning

Error analysis: beyond the stopping point

The car reaches rest after 4 s. The 8 s answer is valid only if the same acceleration continues and makes the car reverse. Ordinary “braking to a stop” ends the model interval at 4 s.
