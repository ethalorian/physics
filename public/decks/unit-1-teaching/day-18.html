Parked or cruising?

A parked truck and a truck moving straight at constant speed are observed over a steady interval. Which has zero net force?

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 185 / 23
Solution and reasoning

Parked or cruising?

Both. Their velocities are constant, so acceleration is zero. A turn or speed change would make the second case different.

Explanation and application

Translational equilibrium

ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣFᵧ = 0

Forces balance in every direction. Velocity stays constant and may be zero.

For extended objects, avoiding rotation also requires torque balance. Today we treat objects as particles or use symmetric arrangements.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1811 / 23
Worked example

A symmetric two-rope setup

A 10 kg sign hangs from two identical ropes, each at 30° above horizontal. The arrangement is symmetric.

Horizontal: T cos30° − T cos30° = 0
Vertical: 2T sin30° − 98 = 0
T = 98 N in each rope
Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1812 / 23
Diagram and reasoning

The two tension vectors

Each 98 N tension contributes 49 N upward. The horizontal components cancel, and the total upward force balances 98 N of weight.

A steeper rope angle

For the same 10 kg sign, each rope is now 60° above horizontal. Find each tension and compare it with the 30° case.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1814 / 23
Solution and reasoning

A steeper rope angle

T = 98 / (2 sin60°) ≈ 56.6 N

Steeper ropes need less tension because more of each tension acts vertically.

Guided equilibrium with two unequal forces

A stationary object has a 12 N force right and a 9 N force upward. A third force balances them. Find its components and magnitude.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1816 / 23
Solution and reasoning

Guided equilibrium with two unequal forces

The third force is (−12, −9) N. Magnitude = √(12² + 9²) = 15 N, directed down and left.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1819 / 23
Explanation and application

Is an orbit equilibrium?

An object travels in a circular orbit at constant speed. Does it have zero net force? Explain using velocity.

Honors Physics / Unit 1 / Day 1820 / 23
Solution and reasoning

Is an orbit equilibrium?

No. Its velocity direction changes, so it accelerates. A nonzero net force points toward the center in the circular model.
