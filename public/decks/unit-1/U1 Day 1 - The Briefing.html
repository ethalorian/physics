SUBJECT: Near-Earth Object 2026-XJ — Initial Trajectory Assessment

1. DISCOVERY. Object 2026-XJ was detected 11 days ago by the ATLAS survey. Follow-up observations from three stations confirm it is on an Earth-approaching trajectory.

2. CURRENT BEST DATA. Distance to Earth: 1.2 × 10⁸ km (± 50,000 km). Velocity: approximately 8,500 m/s (± 200 m/s), directed 15° off the Earth-line. Mass estimate: 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg, inferred from brightness — low confidence.

3. WHAT WE DO NOT KNOW. Composition (rock, metal, or rubble). Rotation state. Whether its path will be significantly bent by solar gravity before arrival. Whether it will hit at all.

4. ASSESSMENT. Impact risk cannot yet be excluded. Preliminary models place the close-approach window approximately one year out. Refined trajectory data expected at each disclosure interval.

5. ACTION. Analysis teams convene today. First position projection due in 22 days. Teams should develop tools for describing motion, predicting position, and quantifying uncertainty.

Which Questions Can Physics Answer?

PHYSICS CAN — MEASURE, MODEL, PREDICT

Where will it be in six months?

How fast is it moving, and is that changing?

How much push would bend its path — and by how much?

How sure are we? (uncertainty is an answer)

TEST: CAN YOU MEASURE IT AND APPLY A LAW TO IT?

PHYSICS CAN'T — PEOPLE DECIDE

Should the public be told? When?

Who pays for a deflection mission?

Is a 1-in-100 risk "worth" acting on?

Which country's cities matter most?

PHYSICS IS THE INPUT. THE DECISION IS YOURS AS A CITIZEN.
