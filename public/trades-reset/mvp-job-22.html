Job 22 · Measure a flow rate

A repeated volume/time investigation with a controlled comparison.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: If arranged: tray, small pitcher, measuring cup, timer, towel, funnel or short tubing; otherwise supplied dataset and paper apparatus model.

Equipment alternative: Analyze supplied trials: baseline 240/12, 230/12, 250/12 mL/s pairs; changed 180/12, 190/12, 170/12. Build and label a paper apparatus model. Mark conclusions as dataset analysis, not a performed wet test.

Brief · 12 min

  1. Which pours faster:300 mL in 12 s or 250 mL in 8 s? Decide using a rate, not volume alone.
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Use a small contained water setup on a tray; the teacher approves the setup and keeps it away from electrical equipment.
  2. Select one collection interval or target volume. Keep starting water level, outlet arrangement, and collection method the same across repeats.
  3. Record at least three trials with volume in mL and time in seconds. Label supplied data if using the paper alternative.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Set up a volume/time table and predict the effect of changing one outlet condition.
  2. Choose only one change and state the controlled starting conditions.
  3. Identify a source of timing or reading uncertainty.

Worked example

A collection is 240 mL in 12 s. Find flow rate and predicted volume in 30 s if the rate stays constant. Flow rate = volume/time; volume = rate×time. 20 mL/s; prediction 600 mL in 30 s. A falling reservoir level can change the rate.

Make · 28 min

  1. Measure the baseline trials, then the changed condition.
  2. Calculate each trial’s rate and compare averages; keep unexpected values visible and explain any repeat.
  3. Draw the setup showing height difference, outlet, and collection point.

Test · 20 min

  1. Volumes and times are actual or explicitly supplied.
  2. The changed condition is separated from controlled conditions.
  3. Explanation connects the observed rate to the setup without claiming water flow and electric current are identical.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A supplied trial collects 350 mL in 14 s. Find rate and predicted time for 500 mL at that constant rate. Why might the prediction fail for an emptying container?
