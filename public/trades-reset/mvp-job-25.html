Job 25 · Improve a thermal design

A revised wrap or envelope model with a controlled retest and material budget.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Previous thermal setup and data; paper and tape; same measuring equipment or supplied comparison.

Equipment alternative: Use supplied original/revised drops 8°C and 6°C at the same stated conditions; analyze the comparison and propose a real retest. Do not record it as student measurement.

Brief · 12 min

  1. Your first test suggests a useful revision. What single feature can you change while keeping the comparison interpretable?
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Review the previous temperature graph and identify a specific weakness or uncertainty.
  2. Choose one change: wrap layers, coverage, or lid condition, with teacher approval.
  3. Record original materials, new materials, and the hypothesis before testing.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Set a fixed paper budget and calculate used area for both versions.
  2. Draw the thermal paths through the sides, lid, and base; mark which one you changed.
  3. Plan the same volume, initial temperature, location, and measurement interval.

Worked example

Original temperature drop is 8°C; revised drop is 6°C over the same interval. Find reduction and percent reduction relative to original drop. Reduction = original−revised; percent = reduction/original×100. 2°C reduction; 25% smaller drop. This is not a 25% increase in R-value.

Make · 28 min

  1. Build the revision and run the comparison or analyze the supplied case.
  2. Calculate and graph change, labeling evidence source.
  3. Revise your claim if variation or uncontrolled conditions prevent a clear conclusion.

Test · 20 min

  1. A single intended variable is identified.
  2. Cost/area and temperature evidence support the recommendation.
  3. A weak or inconclusive result is reported honestly.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A supplied original drop is 5°C and revised drop is 4°C. Find percent reduction. Why is “20% smaller temperature drop” more defensible than “20% better insulation rating”?
