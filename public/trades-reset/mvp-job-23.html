Job 23 · Estimate a thermal envelope

A net-area takeoff and proposed paper room envelope.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Tape, notebook, paper, scissors; model material price card.

Brief · 12 min

  1. A wall has an opening. Should it be counted in covering area, border length, both, or neither? State what is being purchased.
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Survey an approved reachable rectangular classroom wall section and identify openings without climbing.
  2. For the shared model, use a 12 ×8 ft wall with a 3 ×4 ft opening.
  3. Record gross dimensions, opening dimensions, and any unmeasured regions.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Draw the wall and opening at a stated scale.
  2. Create a sheet layout and compare its feasible count with the area lower bound.
  3. Use a fictional price of $6 per sheet; list quantity and subtotal, excluding tax/labor.

Worked example

Find net wall area and sheets if each model sheet covers 8 ft² and arbitrary offcuts can be reused in this area-only estimate. Net area = gross area − openings; lower-bound sheet count = ceiling(net/sheet area). 96−12=84 ft²; 84/8=10.5, so at least 11 sheets. An actual cutting layout may require more.

Make · 28 min

  1. Build a paper wall section with the opening and sheet seams.
  2. Have another pair trace each quantity to a dimension.
  3. Revise one seam layout to reduce offcuts or improve continuity.

Test · 20 min

  1. Gross, opening, and net areas are distinct.
  2. Purchase count follows a feasible layout, with any waste stated.
  3. The model is a material proposal, not a claim of measured insulation performance.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A 10 ×9 ft wall has a 2 ×3 ft opening. Find net area and area-based minimum number of 12-ft² sheets. Explain why a cut layout is still needed.
