Job 04 · Estimate a display surface

A covering-and-border takeoff with a piece layout and simulated quote.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Tape, notebook, paper, scissors, pencil, calculator after setting up the math.

Brief · 12 min

  1. Point to the surface and then its boundary. Which purchase uses square units and which uses length?
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Survey a reachable rectangular display area. Record width/height in inches, then convert to feet.
  2. For a shared purchasing exercise, use a model surface 4 × 6 ft. Paper rectangles will represent materials at 1 in = 1 ft.
  3. Separate covering area from border length in two labeled notebook sections.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Draw and cut the scaled panels and surface. Test that the panel orientation covers the rectangle.
  2. Make a cutting plan for two 6-ft and two 4-ft border pieces using three 8-ft strips. Show leftovers.
  3. List item, size, quantity, unit price, subtotal, and total. Prices are fictional classroom data, excluding tax and labor.

Worked example

Cover a 4 × 6 ft surface using 2 × 3 ft panels at $4 each. Border comes in 8-ft strips at $3 each. Assume no cutting loss in this paper model. Area = L × W; perimeter = 2 L + 2 W. Count whole purchasable pieces after checking layout. Area 24 ft²; four panels tile the surface. Perimeter 20 ft; three 8-ft strips supply 24 ft. Cost = 4×$4 + 3×$3 = $25.

Make · 28 min

  1. Assemble a paper proof of the panel layout. Keep offcuts visible.
  2. Have another pair recalculate quantities from your drawing.
  3. Repeat the takeoff for your measured surface using the same model materials; state any simplifying assumption.

Test · 20 min

  1. Panel count is supported by a layout, not area division alone.
  2. The stock plan can actually supply each border piece; no piece is longer than stock.
  3. Quote totals and all units can be traced to the drawing.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A 3 × 5 ft board uses 1 × 1 ft covering squares at $0.50 each. Border is sold in 6-ft strips at $2 each; show a feasible plan for two 5-ft and two 3-ft edges. Find covering, border purchase, and total cost.
