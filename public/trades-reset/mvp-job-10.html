Job 10 · Compare two material quotes

Two feasible estimates and a recommendation supported by quantities and waste.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Paper, notebook, calculator, fictional price card supplied in the lesson.

Brief · 12 min

  1. A cheaper unit price can still lead to a more expensive job. What must we compare besides the price label?
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Use a model 6 × 8 ft covering job.
  2. Option A panels: 2 × 4 ft at $7 each. Option B panels: 3 × 4 ft at $11 each. Both can be rotated; use no cutting loss for this comparison.
  3. Draw scaled rectangles for the surface and each material.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Create both piece layouts and itemized quotes.
  2. Calculate purchased area minus required area for each option; label waste in square feet.
  3. Recommend an option using both cost and fit. State which real-world costs were omitted.

Worked example

Compare purchase cost for the 6 × 8 ft job. Purchased quantity follows a feasible tiling. Total = quantity × unit price. A: six panels × $7 = $42. B: four panels × $11 = $44. Both tile 48 ft² exactly; A saves $2 on this model.

Make · 28 min

  1. Exchange quote packages and audit each other’s multiplication and piece layout.
  2. Teacher changes the surface to 5 × 8 ft. Re-plan cuts for both options.
  3. Keep the original and revised quotes so a user can see why the recommendation changes or stays.

Test · 20 min

  1. Every quantity is supported by a layout.
  2. Fictional prices and omitted tax/labor are labeled.
  3. Recommendation names a numerical difference rather than saying one is simply better.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A job needs 18 ft². Option X gives 6 ft² per sheet at $5; Option Y gives 8 ft² per sheet at $7. Assuming both piece layouts work, find purchase quantities, costs, and unused purchased area.
