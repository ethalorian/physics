Job 28 · Diagnose a circuit fault

A fault-finding record showing prediction, test, result, and repair.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Approved low-voltage kit with teacher-planted open connection or switch fault; meter only if taught; paper fault cards as fallback.

Equipment alternative: Use two paper diagrams, one with an open switch and one broken lead. Specify and explain the distinguishing inspection; do not claim a measured repair.

Brief · 12 min

  1. The lamp is dark. Why is replacing every part at once a weak diagnostic method?
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Compare the circuit to its approved diagram with power disconnected.
  2. List two plausible causes: an open connection or an open switch/contact.
  3. Choose a test that separates the causes without shorting the source.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Write a hypothesis and expected observation for one test.
  2. Plan inspection/continuity tracing first; use a meter only in a mode demonstrated by the teacher.
  3. Prepare a four-column log: hypothesis, test, observation, next action.

Worked example

A supplied resistor test has 6 V across a 30-Ω load. After replacement with 60 Ω at the same voltage, predict current. I=V/R. 0.20 A becomes 0.10 A. A current change is not by itself proof that the original fault was resistance.

Make · 28 min

  1. Make one change or test at a time with appropriate source disconnection.
  2. Repair the identified open path and demonstrate operation.
  3. Restore the original drawing with the verified connection labeled; explain evidence against one alternative cause.

Test · 20 min

  1. Log shows an ordered diagnostic path.
  2. Conclusion cites a test observation, not only a guess.
  3. Circuit completes three functional cycles after repair, or the paper alternative identifies the complete path after correction.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

A 6-V ideal circuit with total resistance 40 Ω should carry what current? If the switch is open, why should you investigate continuity before using that value as an actual reading?
