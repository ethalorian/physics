Job 24 · Compare heat loss

A controlled temperature-time comparison and evidence-based conclusion.

110 minutes • Tape measure + pocket notebook • Name: __________________ Date: __________

Materials: Arranged in advance: two matching cups/containers, thermometers, timer, safe teacher-supplied warm water, paper wrap, tray; or supplied dataset.

Equipment alternative: Use supplied times 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 min; A 40, 38, 36.5, 35.5, 34.5, 34°C; B 40, 39, 38, 37.2, 36.6, 36°C. Build paper wrap models; label graphs supplied-data analysis.

Brief · 12 min

  1. If one cup starts hotter, is its final temperature alone a fair test of insulation?
  2. Set up a dated notebook spread: sketch and readings on the left; calculation, decision, test, and revision on the right. Mark small sketches NTS unless drawn to a chosen scale.
  3. Work in pairs and rotate roles. Each student keeps an individual notebook and performs a tool action and a calculation.

Measure · 18 min

  1. Use two matching containers with equal water volume and similar initial temperature, located together.
  2. Wrap one with a documented paper layer and leave the other as the comparison. Keep lid condition and measurement method the same.
  3. Measure initial temperature and then at 2-min intervals through 10 min; record room temperature if a suitable thermometer is available.

Plan · 20 min

  1. Predict which arrangement will show a smaller drop and why.
  2. Create a time/temperature table and label the variable changed.
  3. Plan a repeat or a limitation statement if only one trial per condition is possible.

Worked example

A supplied cup changes from 40°C to 34°C in 10 min. Find temperature drop and average cooling magnitude. Drop = initial−final; average cooling magnitude = drop/time. 6°C drop; 0.6°C/min average over that interval, not necessarily the rate at every instant.

Make · 28 min

  1. Run the comparison without changing wrap or lids midway.
  2. Graph both temperature histories on the same labeled axes.
  3. Compare temperature drops and discuss measurement uncertainty or uneven starts.

Test · 20 min

  1. Temperatures are measured or explicitly supplied.
  2. The conclusion uses change over the same time and comparable starting conditions.
  3. Students do not calculate a material R-value from this simple cup test.

Explain · 12 min

  1. Complete the individual exit below before discussing answers.
  2. Add the test result, revision, and notebook page reference to your response. Use the notebook as evidence rather than retyping every page.
  3. Save the three selected responses and submit the lesson for teacher review. Show your physical work and notebook to the teacher. Clean up and return shared supplies.

Individual exit

Supplied cups start at 42°C. After 8 min, A is 36°C and B is 38°C. Find each average cooling magnitude and identify which retained more thermal energy if water masses are equal. Name one controlled condition.
