## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Your turn.** F_net = 30 − 10 = **20 N forward**; a = 20 ÷ 5 = **4 m/s²**. Gravity and normal cancel vertically.

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** Only the part of gravity *along* the slope pulls the block down it: **mg sin θ**. The part perpendicular to the slope, mg cos θ, presses it into the ramp (and is balanced by the normal force). The next card draws the tilted axes and both dashed components.

Which function? The angle between F_g and the perpendicular axis equals the ramp angle θ. The component **opposite** θ (along the slope) uses sin; the one **adjacent** (into the slope) uses cos. This is the Day 6 rule.

**Worked example (on screen).** m = 2 kg, θ = 30°, friction 4.0 N up the slope: mg = 19.6 N; down-slope 9.8 N; into-slope 17.0 N = F_N; F_net = 9.8 − 4.0 = 5.8 N; a = 2.9 m/s².

**STOP 3 · Pairs.** Frictionless 20° ramp: a = g sin 20° = 9.8 × 0.342 ≈ **3.4 m/s²**. The mass cancels (F_net = mg sin θ, a = F_net/m). Ask: "Would a 10 kg block be faster?" (No.)

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** Earth's gravity (toward Earth's centre) plus engine thrust (forward). F_net is their vector sum, so the spacecraft both curves (gravity) and speeds up (thrust). No normal force, no friction.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** F_net = 50 − 15 = **35 N forward** (a = 3.5 m/s²).

## Formative checks
- **Inclines:** are students using mg sin θ along and mg cos θ into the slope? Swapping them is the most common error.
- **Vector addition:** are students choosing a positive direction and subtracting opposing forces?
- **Exit ticket:** 35 N forward.
