## Stops and answers

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** 4 m forward, 1 m back. Distance = 5 m (scalar: total ground covered). Displacement = +3 m (vector: start to finish). Answers appear when you continue.

**STOP 2 · Today's question.** Same start, same finish, so same displacement, but car A takes half the time. Let students argue it out; the next section builds speed and velocity from their answers.

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 20 m/s. Shortcut: ÷ 3.6.

**STOP 4 · Your turn.** 8,500 m/s × 3.6 ≈ 30,600 km/h. 8,500 m/s × 2.237 ≈ 19,000 mph. About 35× a jet airliner (~550 mph); about 290× highway speed.

**STOP 5 · Investigation 1.1 (Match-the-Graph).** Vernier motion detector on the laptop. Start with constant-velocity targets, then targets that change direction (negative velocity). Predict, attempt, observe, refine.

**STOP 6 · Discuss.** *How would you walk to make each graph?* Steep: walk away quickly. Shallow: walk away slowly. Flat: stand still. Push on the flat one: it means **at rest**, not constant speed. Constant speed is a straight sloped line.

**STOP 7 · Discuss.** The answer appears when you continue. With the origin at the far wall and + toward the sensor, the same walk has **positive** velocity. The sign depends on the axis you chose. Negative velocity means motion in the negative coordinate direction.

**STOP 8 · Exit ticket.** Expect: axes labelled position (m) and time (s), a straight line sloping **upward**, and the slope labelled velocity (constant, positive).

## Formative checks
- **Match-the-graph:** do students improve over attempts? Those who can't match by the end may need support.
- **Exit ticket:** check the named evidence and reasoning. A correct-looking sketch without labels or a slope explanation is incomplete evidence.
- **Misconception:** a flat line on a position–time graph means *not moving*, not "constant speed."

Note: the 2026-XJ graph in the last section uses illustrative data. Its slope (≈ −8,500 m/s) matches the speed used earlier in the lesson.
