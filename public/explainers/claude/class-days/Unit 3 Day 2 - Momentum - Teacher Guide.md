## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Car.** p = 1500 × 30 = **45,000 kg·m/s east**. Insist on units and direction.

**Worked sequence (on screen, all east).**
| Object | m (kg) | v (m/s) | p (kg·m/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| car | 1500 | 30 | 45,000 |
| bullet | 0.05 | 400 | 20 |
| sprinter | 70 | 10 | 700 |
| cruise ship | 5 × 10⁶ | 8 | 4 × 10⁷ |
| 2026-XJ | 3.5 × 10¹¹ | 8,500 | 3.0 × 10¹⁵ |

The bullet is fast but has tiny momentum; the ship is slow but has huge momentum.

**STOP 2 · Predict.** 2026-XJ: p ≈ 3.5 × 10¹¹ × 8,500 ≈ **3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg·m/s**, about **75 million** cruise ships (3.0 × 10¹⁵ ÷ 4 × 10⁷ ≈ 7.5 × 10⁷). The film uses the same scenario mass and speed as earlier units; substitute your class values if they differ.

**STOP 3 · Practice (packet).** Students choose a + direction first and keep the sign on every velocity.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** East = +. p_i = 0.15 × (+40) = **+6.0 kg·m/s**; p_f = 0.15 × (−50) = **−7.5 kg·m/s**. Δp = p_f − p_i = −7.5 − 6.0 = **−13.5 kg·m/s** (13.5 kg·m/s west). The common wrong answer is −1.5 or 1.5 kg·m/s, from subtracting speeds (50 − 40) instead of signed momenta.

**STOP 5 · Marzano on K1 and S1.**

## Formative checks
- Signed velocities and kg·m/s on every answer.
- In the reversal, students subtract signed momenta, not speed magnitudes.
