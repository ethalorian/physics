## Stops and answers

**STOP 1 · Warm-up.** *To predict where something will be later, what three things do you need to know?*
Current position, velocity (speed and direction), and how much time passes. Assumption: velocity stays constant.

**STOP 2 · Prep check.** *Convert 8,210 m/s to km/s. What does the 7 in 1.6 × 10⁷ s tell you?*
8.210 km/s. The 7 means move the decimal 7 places: 16,000,000 s, about 185 days. If students struggle, replay only the needed part of Video 10 (scientific notation) or Video 12 (unit conversions).

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** *Find Δx and Δy over 6 months, then the new position.*
Δx = (−8.210 km/s)(1.6 × 10⁷ s) = −1.314 × 10⁸ km. Δy = (2.200 km/s)(1.6 × 10⁷ s) = +3.52 × 10⁷ km.
x = 1.2 × 10⁸ + (−1.314 × 10⁸) = −1.14 × 10⁷ km; y = 3.52 × 10⁷ km.

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** *Closer to Earth than now? Which side?*
Closer: d = √(x² + y²) ≈ 3.70 × 10⁷ km, versus 1.2 × 10⁸ km now. Negative x means it has passed to the far side of Earth (in the x direction), off to the +y side. Stress: constant-velocity model, not an orbit prediction.

**STOP 5 · Your turn.** *Find the range of possible x and y; sketch the window.*
Velocity: ±0.200 km/s × 1.6 × 10⁷ s = ±3.2 × 10⁶ km. Position: ±5.0 × 10⁴ km. Total ≈ ±3.25 × 10⁶ km in each direction.
x: −1.46 × 10⁷ to −0.81 × 10⁷ km; y: 3.20 × 10⁷ to 3.85 × 10⁷ km.

**STOP 6 · Discuss.** *Which uncertainty matters more?*
Velocity, by about 64× (3.2 × 10⁶ vs 5 × 10⁴ km). Velocity error is multiplied by time, so it keeps growing; position error doesn't.

**STOP 7 · Discuss.** *Why will the real window be bigger?*
We assumed constant velocity. The Sun's gravity changes the velocity, bending the path, so our straight-line model underestimates the spread (Unit 2).

**STOP 8 · Exit ticket.** *Why a window instead of one exact location?*
Look for: measurements have inherent limits (not guessing); those limits carry through the calculation, and velocity uncertainty grows with time; plus the model itself is simplified.

## Formative checks
- **Units:** watch for km vs m mixing. It's the most common error (answers off by 1000×).
- **Uncertainty reasoning:** "uncertainty" means measurement limits, not guessing.
- **Exit ticket:** students should say data has noise and the noise propagates through the calculation.

Note: 2026-XJ is a fictional classroom scenario; the measurement, prediction and uncertainty methods are real.
