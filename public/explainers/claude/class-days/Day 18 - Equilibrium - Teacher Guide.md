## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Discuss.** Parked car: **yes**. Cruising straight at a steady speed: **yes** (constant velocity). Turning at 25 m/s: **no**, its direction is changing, so it's accelerating. Ball at the top: **no**, gravity is the only force (Day 15). Students often say the turning car is in equilibrium "because its speed is constant."

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** Constant velocity → ΣF_x = 0, so friction = **12 N**. ΣF_y = 0, so F_N = mg = 3 × 9.8 = **29.4 N**.

**STOP 3 · Pairs (20–43 min).** 2T sin 60° = 98 N → T = 98 / (2 × 0.866) ≈ **56.6 N**. **Steeper ropes need less tension**: more of each rope's pull points up. At 30° each rope carries 98 N, the sign's full weight. Use this stop for your own two-rope and wall-sign problems.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** T = mg = 5 × 9.8 = **49 N**.

## Unit 2 foreshadow (no stop)
2026-XJ isn't in equilibrium: the Sun's gravity always pulls on it. That acceleration is small, so over minutes or hours its velocity barely changes, and constant velocity is a good approximation. Over months it isn't, which is Unit 2's problem.

## Formative checks
- **Equilibrium problems:** do students write ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0 explicitly?
- **Multi-rope problems:** are tensions split with sin for the vertical part and cos for the horizontal (angle measured from horizontal)?
- **Exit ticket:** T = 49 N.
