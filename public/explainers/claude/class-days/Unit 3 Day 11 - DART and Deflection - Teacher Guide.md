## DART facts (for reference)
DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test) hit Dimorphos, the ~160 m moonlet of asteroid Didymos, on 26 September 2022. Its orbit period dropped from **11 h 55 min to about 11 h 23 min (≈ 32–33 min shorter)**. The spacecraft was ~580 kg at ~6.1 km/s; Dimorphos is roughly 4–5 × 10⁹ kg. The film rounds to the plan's 600 kg, 6 km/s and 5 × 10⁹ kg.

## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Video.** Show your 1-minute mission animation here.

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** Perfectly inelastic, Dimorphos effectively at rest: mv = (m + M)Δv.
- p = (600)(6000) = **3.6 × 10⁶ kg·m/s**
- Δv = 3.6 × 10⁶ / (5 × 10⁹) ≈ **7 × 10⁻⁴ m/s ≈ 0.7 mm/s**

**Important nuance (on screen).** The *measured* change was about **2.7 mm/s**, several times more than this simple estimate. Rock and dust blasted off the surface (ejecta) carried momentum backward, so by conservation the asteroid got an extra forward push. Our model leaves that out; it's a great "what did we assume?" discussion.

**Why tiny matters (on screen).** A small Δv shifts position by drift = Δv × t: 0.7 mm/s for 10 years (3.2 × 10⁸ s) ≈ 220 km. Simple straight-line model.

**STOP 3 · Group problem (class numbers).** 2026-XJ m = 1 × 10⁹ kg; shift by one Earth radius (6.37 × 10⁶ m) with 6 months (≈ 1.6 × 10⁷ s) lead time.
- Δv = 6.37 × 10⁶ / 1.6 × 10⁷ ≈ **0.40 m/s**
- Δp = (1 × 10⁹)(0.40) = **4 × 10⁸ kg·m/s**
- ≈ 4 × 10⁸ / 3.6 × 10⁶ ≈ **110 DART-sized hits** (about 30 with DART's real ejecta boost)

Note: this uses the plan's 1 × 10⁹ kg. Earlier units used 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg for 2026-XJ; at that mass the same goal needs ~350× more momentum. That's a strong discussion point.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** Sample: "DART gave Dimorphos Δv ≈ 0.7 mm/s (mv = (m + M)Δv). I assumed it was perfectly inelastic and ignored the ejecta."

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** One DART on 10⁹ kg gives ~3.6 mm/s; we need ~0.40 m/s, about 110× more. Warning time is the lever: drift = Δv × t, so 10 years instead of 6 months needs about 20× less Δv. Real planetary defense focuses on finding asteroids early. In real orbits, an early along-track nudge is even more effective than the straight-line model suggests.

## Formative checks
- Momentum transfer, and a small Δv accumulating a displacement over time.
- The simple model's assumptions stated (perfectly inelastic, no ejecta, straight-line drift) before scaling to a real orbit.
