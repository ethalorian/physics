## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** 1 kg: 9.81 N. 2 kg: 19.6 N. Force per kg: **9.81 N/kg for both**. That per-kilogram number doesn't depend on the test mass; it's the field.

**The field move (on screen).** g = F/m = GM/r². Units: N/kg = (kg·m/s²)/kg = m/s².

**STOP 2 · Predict.** Most students guess "close to zero." Answer (top of the next card's table): **8.69 m/s²**, about 89% of the surface value.

**Worked examples** (GM = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ ≈ 3.98 × 10¹⁴ m³/s²):
| Place | r (m) | g (m/s²) |
|---|---|---|
| Earth's surface | 6.37 × 10⁶ | 9.81 |
| ISS (≈ 400 km up) | 6.77 × 10⁶ | 8.69 |
| Moon's orbit | 3.84 × 10⁸ | 2.7 × 10⁻³ |
| 2026-XJ now (1.2 × 10⁸ km) | 1.2 × 10¹¹ | 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ |

Note: your plan's M = 6 × 10²⁴ kg gives 9.9 at the surface; the film uses 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg so the surface value comes out 9.81.

**STOP 3 · Class table (28–40 min).** Half strength: r = √2 R ≈ 9.0 × 10⁶ m → altitude ≈ **2,600 km**. 1% strength: r = 10 R ≈ 6.4 × 10⁷ m → altitude ≈ **57,000 km**. The graph and answers play after the stop. Watch for students using altitude instead of distance from Earth's centre.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** Gravity at the ISS is still about 8.7 m/s². Astronauts float because the ISS and everyone in it are in **free fall together**, falling around Earth, so nothing pushes up on them. "Zero gravity" is the wrong name: it's shared free fall.
