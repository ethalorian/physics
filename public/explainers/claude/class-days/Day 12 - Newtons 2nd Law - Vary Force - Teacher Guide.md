## What to listen for at each stop

**STOP 1 · Discuss.** Change the force (the hanging mass); keep the cart's mass the same. The next card opens with this answer.

**STOP 2 · Predict.** Have students sketch a vs F before any data. Most predict "more force, more acceleration"; push for the *shape* (straight? curved? through zero?).

**STOP 3 · Lab (15–40 min).** Leave paused while groups run 4–5 hanging masses. Remind them F ≈ m × g with m in kg (50 g → 0.49 N). Watch for g vs kg errors.

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** Use your class data. The next card reveals the example result: a straight line through the origin; doubling F roughly doubles a.

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** Double the force (or push for twice as long; the Day 11 trade-off). With m ≈ 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg, 1,000 N gives 2.9 × 10⁻⁹ m/s² and 2,000 N gives 5.7 × 10⁻⁹ m/s².

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** The acceleration would **double**.

## Example data (0.50 kg cart)
| hanging mass (g) | F = mg (N) | a (m/s²) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.098 | 0.190 |
| 20 | 0.196 | 0.379 |
| 30 | 0.294 | 0.552 |
| 40 | 0.392 | 0.728 |
| 50 | 0.490 | 0.889 |

Slope ≈ 1.8 kg⁻¹, so 1/slope ≈ 0.55 kg. That's more than the 0.50 kg cart because the hanging mass accelerates too: the force mg moves cart *plus* hanger. Students who plot against the force sensor's reading (string tension) instead get a slope close to 1/(0.50 kg) = 2.0 kg⁻¹.

## Formative checks
- **Lab data:** slopes positive and increasing with hanging mass? Values within roughly 0.1–1 m/s² for these masses?
- **Synthesis:** at the Combine card, can students say how a ∝ 1/m (Day 11) and a ∝ F (Day 12) give F = ma?
- **Exit ticket:** "acceleration would double."
