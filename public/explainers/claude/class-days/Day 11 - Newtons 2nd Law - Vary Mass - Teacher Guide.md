## What to listen for at each stop

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Students should commit to a testable prediction ("a heavier cart speeds up more slowly"). Push for a direction *and* a guess at how much.

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** Independent variable: cart mass. Dependent: acceleration (from the v–t slope). Controlled: the hanging mass, so the pulling force stays the same. The answer opens the next card.

**STOP 3 · Lab (15–40 min).** Leave the film paused while groups run 3–4 masses. The film modeled one trial: 0.50 kg, slope ≈ 0.37 m/s².

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** Use your class's data. The next card reveals the example trend: more mass, less acceleration, as a curve; doubling m roughly halves a; a vs 1/m is a straight line; m·a is about constant.

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** Either works in principle; the trade-off is time. With m ≈ 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg, a 1,000 N push gives a ≈ 2.9 × 10⁻⁹ m/s², or Δv ≈ 0.9 m/s after 10 years. A short, huge push (an impactor) needs far less warning time; a gentle push needs years of lead time.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** The acceleration would **halve** (a = F/m).

## Example data (20 g hanging mass, 0.196 N weight)
| m (kg) | a (m/s²) | 1/m (1/kg) | m·a (N) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 0.374 | 2.00 | 0.187 |
| 0.75 | 0.257 | 1.33 | 0.193 |
| 1.00 | 0.190 | 1.00 | 0.190 |
| 1.25 | 0.155 | 0.80 | 0.194 |

m·a sits a little below 0.196 N because the hanging mass accelerates too, so the string pulls slightly less than its weight. It creeps up slightly with heavier carts for the same reason.

## Formative checks
- **Lab data:** values should fall as mass rises; a group whose acceleration *rises* with mass likely mislabeled trials or changed the hanging mass.
- **Graphing:** can students see the curve in a vs m, and the straight line in a vs 1/m?
- **Exit ticket:** "acceleration would halve."
