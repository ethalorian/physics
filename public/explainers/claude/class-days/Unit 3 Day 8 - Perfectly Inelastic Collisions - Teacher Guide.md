## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** Use your class's Velcro numbers; most groups should be within a few percent.

**Setup (on screen).** When objects stick, they share one final velocity. Σp before = Σp after gives m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v_f, so v_f = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂)/(m₁ + m₂). It's just conservation of momentum with one unknown.

**STOP 2 · Bullet into a block.** (0.05)(400) + (5)(0) = (5.05)v_f → v_f = 20 ÷ 5.05 ≈ **3.96 m/s**, in the bullet's direction. Watch for students who divide by 5 instead of 5.05 (forgetting the bullet is now part of the moving mass).

**STOP 3 · Head-on cars.** East = +. (1200)(+15) + (1500)(−10) = 2700 v_f → 18,000 − 15,000 = 3,000 → v_f ≈ **+1.11 m/s, i.e. 1.1 m/s east**. Check: it lies between +15 and −10. The wreck moves east because the 1200 kg car had more momentum (18,000 vs 15,000 kg·m/s), even though it's the lighter car. For force-direction reasoning: during the crash each car pushes the other with equal and opposite force (Newton's 3rd Law), so each car's Δp is equal and opposite.

**STOP 4 · Practice.** Three worksheet problems. Three rules: one shared v_f, the total mass, a sign on every velocity.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** (1000)(+20) + (2000)(0) = (3000)v_f → v_f ≈ **+6.7 m/s (6.7 m/s east)**.
