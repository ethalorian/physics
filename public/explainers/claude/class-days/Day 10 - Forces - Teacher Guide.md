## What to listen for at each stop

**STOP 1 · Discuss.** Collect everyday examples (pushing a door, a backpack strap, gravity). The next card formalizes: a push or a pull, measured in newtons, about 1 N for a small apple's weight. Forces are vectors: magnitude and direction.

**STOP 2 · Predict.** Run the live force-sensor demo here, then continue. The film shows a pull reading positive and a push reading negative (the Go Direct Force and Acceleration sensor's default sign). The five force cards follow.

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** Gravity down, normal up, equal in length. The answer opens the next card.

**STOP 4 · Pairs.** The film models a car on a road (gravity, normal, applied/engine forward, friction + drag backward) and a person on a rope (gravity, tension), then warns that "momentum," "speed," and "inertia" are not forces. Use your own practice scenarios at this stop.

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** Humans can add an applied force. The next card shows a kinetic impactor (NASA's DART spacecraft really struck the asteroid Dimorphos in September 2022) and a nuclear option (a brief, enormous push from a nearby blast).

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** Gravity (straight down), normal force (perpendicular to the hill's surface, not straight up), and friction plus air drag (up the hill, opposing motion). There is no applied force: the engine is off. Gravity is what makes the car speed up downhill.

## Notes on the asteroid card
- At roughly Earth's distance from the Sun, solar gravity on 2026-XJ is about 0.006 m/s² of acceleration; Earth's pull at 1.2 × 10⁸ km is about 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ m/s² (Day 7), so roughly 200,000 times weaker right now. The bars are labeled "not to scale."
- Solar radiation pressure is real and measurable for small asteroids over years (it's one input to the Yarkovsky effect), but tiny compared with gravity.

## Formative checks
- **Complete force lists:** watch for missing gravity, missing normal force, and invented forces ("momentum," "the force of motion").
- **Arrows:** proportional lengths and correct directions, especially the normal force on a slope.
- **Exit ticket:** gravity, normal, friction/drag, each drawn as an arrow, with no engine force.
