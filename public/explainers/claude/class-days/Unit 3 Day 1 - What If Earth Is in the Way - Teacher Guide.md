## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Students predict trial 2 (same light car, fast) and trial 3 (heavier cart, slow). Both buckle the wall.

**STOP 2 · Notice & wonder.** Lead the class to two things: **how much stuff is moving (mass)** and **how fast it's moving (velocity)**. The next card names the single number that combines them: momentum, p = mv, in kg·m/s. Keep it a tease; tomorrow is the full treatment.

**STOP 3 · Video (optional).** DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test) hit Dimorphos, the small moonlet of asteroid Didymos, on 26 September 2022. Dimorphos's orbit period dropped from 11 h 55 min to about 11 h 23 min (≈32 minutes shorter). Its speed changed by only a few **millimetres** per second (about 2.7 mm/s), not centimetres. The film's orbit shrink is exaggerated so it's visible. Ask: would a nudge that small work on 2026-XJ? (Tomorrow's answer starts with: it depends on its momentum and how much warning time there is.)

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.**
- Cart A: p = 1 × 2 = 2 kg·m/s; cart B: p = 3 × 2 = 6 kg·m/s. Both stop, so Δp = −2 and −6 kg·m/s.
- **Cart B** needs the larger change in momentum and, because both stop in the **same** 1 s, the larger average stopping force (about 6 N vs 2 N).
- Controlled comparison: same velocity, same stopping time; only mass changed.
- Cart A at +4 m/s: p = 4 kg·m/s, Δp = −4 kg·m/s, force ≈ 4 N: larger than before, still less than B.
- Sample asteroid question: "How much would we need to change 2026-XJ's momentum, and how early?"
