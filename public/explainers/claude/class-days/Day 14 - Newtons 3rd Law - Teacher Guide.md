## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Argue it (2 min).** Let it run. Common wrong answers: "the horse pulls harder" and "the forces cancel." Collect them; STOP 2 and the next card resolve both.

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** They don't cancel because they act on **different objects**: one on the cart, one on the horse. The next card shows each object's own forces. The cart speeds up because the horse's pull on it beats friction; the horse speeds up because the ground's push on its hooves beats the cart's pull back.

**STOP 3 · Lab (15–35 min).** Leave paused while pairs run the tug-of-war. Set both sensors to the same sign convention so one reads + and the other −.

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** **Neither:** the forces are equal at every instant, even with a much stronger student. The stronger student "wins" because of the ground's push on *their* feet, not because they pull harder on the rope. The answer appears at the top of the next card.

**STOP 5 · Discuss.** a = F/m. Equal forces, but 2026-XJ has about 6 × 10⁸ times the spacecraft's mass, so its change in velocity is tiny while the spacecraft's is huge (it stops and breaks up). That's fine: the mission's job is to transfer motion, not survive. (Momentum, coming later, makes this precise.) Answer at the top of the exit card.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** **The wall.** It pushes back on your hands with a force equal in size and opposite in direction.

## Numbers
- Spacecraft ≈ 600 kg (similar to NASA's DART, which hit Dimorphos in 2022). 2026-XJ ≈ 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg. Mass ratio ≈ 6 × 10⁸.

## Formative checks
- **Tug-of-war:** do students say the sensors *confirm* the 3rd Law?
- **Misconception:** anyone still claiming the stronger student pulls harder?
- **Exit ticket:** "the wall."
