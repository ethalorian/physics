## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Quick check.** Mass: how much stuff, in kg, the same everywhere. Weight: gravity's pull on it, in N (F = mg). It changed on Mars because Mars's g is smaller (3.71 vs 9.81 m/s²).

**Direct instruction (on screen).** Each piece of F = G m₁ m₂ / r². G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² is tiny, which is why gravity is negligible between everyday objects. Why r²: the same "lines of pull" spread over a sphere whose surface area grows as r².

**STOP 2 · Predict.** Get all three predictions written before continuing.

**STOP 3 · PhET Gravity Force Lab (18–32 min).** Double m₁ → **2F**; double r → **F/4**; triple r → **F/9**. The most common error: "double r → F/2." Return to the sphere picture: area × 4, pull ÷ 4.

**Worked example (no stop).**
- Earth (6 × 10²⁴ kg) and a 1000 kg asteroid at 384,000 km = 3.84 × 10⁸ m: F = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(6 × 10²⁴)(1000) / (3.84 × 10⁸)² ≈ **2.7 N**, about the weight of a can of soup.
- Day 3's question: at Earth's surface (r = 6.37 × 10⁶ m), g = GM/r² ≈ 9.9 m/s² with M = 6 × 10²⁴ kg (9.81 with the exact 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg). So 2026-XJ on a giant scale would read about (3.5 × 10¹¹)(9.81) ≈ **3.4 × 10¹² N**.

**STOP 4 · Exit ticket.** Doubles its distance → force is **¼** as large. Triples → **⅑**.
