## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Friction. Ice has very little, the floor a lot. The next card pushes further: what if there were none?

**STOP 2 · Discuss.** Listen for "it keeps doing what it's doing unless something changes it." **Net force** = the total of all the forces; balanced forces add to zero, so the motion doesn't change. Note the card's wording says "at constant velocity": same speed *and* direction. Galileo's argument is shown as a thought experiment, which matches how it's usually presented.

**STOP 3 · Predict (tablecloth).** The cups stay put. The cloth exerts friction on them only for a very short time, so their velocity barely changes: inertia.

**STOP 4 · Predict (coin and card).** The card shoots sideways; the coin stays where it was and drops straight into the glass.

**STOP 5 · Discuss (car).** No force pushes the passenger forward. The car slows; the passenger's body keeps moving at the old velocity (inertia) until something applies a net force: the seatbelt. Watch for "the stop threw me forward," which treats inertia as a force.

**Asteroid section.** In space there's no air or friction, so with no net force 2026-XJ would coast at constant velocity. What does act on it is gravity, mostly the Sun's, plus weaker pulls from Earth and the Moon. To change its path, we need a force: predictable, but hard to stop.

**STOP 6 · Exit ticket.** Strong answers: by the 1st Law, the asteroid's velocity won't change unless a net force acts on it; it keeps coming because nothing is stopping it, so a mission must push on it to change its motion.

## Formative checks
- **Exit ticket:** do students say that motion doesn't change without a (net) force?
- **Demo predictions:** right, or surprised? Surprise is a good moment to ask "what did you expect, and why?"
- **Vocabulary:** can students use "inertia" and "net force" correctly in a sentence? Inertia is not a force.
