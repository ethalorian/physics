## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Collect ideas. The next card names them: static friction (before it moves) can grow up to a maximum; once sliding, kinetic friction is usually smaller.

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** N = mg = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N. To start: push must exceed μ_s N = **49 N**. To keep it at constant speed: push = μ_k N = **29.4 N**.

**STOP 3 · Lab (15–35 min).** Pull at constant velocity so a = 0 and the sensor reading equals friction. A wheeled cart has very little friction, so attach a friction block or pad underneath (or use a wooden block) to get readings like the example data.

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** **No.** f and N both doubled, so μ_k = f/N stayed 0.30. μ depends on the surface pair, not on mass or (within our range) speed.

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** *Easier:* nothing slows a pushed asteroid, so a small force kept up for a long time keeps adding velocity. *Harder:* nothing slows the asteroid naturally either; it keeps coming unless we act.
