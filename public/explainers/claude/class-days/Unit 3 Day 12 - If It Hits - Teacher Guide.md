## Scenario values
The film uses the class scenario values from earlier units: **m = 3.5 × 10¹¹ kg, v = 8,500 m/s**. (Real impact speeds are at least 11 km/s, because Earth's gravity speeds an asteroid up as it falls in; substitute your class values if they differ.)

## Answers and what to listen for

**STOP 1 · Predict.** Its momentum (and then the impulse it delivers). Accept energy too; it's tomorrow's tease.

**STOP 2 · Your turn.** p = mv = (3.5 × 10¹¹)(8,500) ≈ **3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg·m/s**.

**STOP 3 · Your turn.** It stops, so Δp ≈ −3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg·m/s. With Δt ≈ 0.1 s: F = Δp/Δt ≈ **3 × 10¹⁶ N** (on the ground; equal and opposite on the asteroid). The Empire State Building weighs about 3 × 10⁹ N, so this is roughly **10 million** of them. Key point on screen: the answer **depends on the assumed Δt**: 0.01 s → 3 × 10¹⁷ N, 1 s → 3 × 10¹⁵ N. Same impulse every time.

**STOP 4 · Discuss.** An average force says how hard, on average, while the asteroid stops. It doesn't capture **heat** (vaporised rock), the **shockwave**, **crater size**, or the **energy delivered**. It also depends on a guessed Δt and ignores the area hit and the materials involved. Unit 4 preview on screen: KE = ½mv² ≈ 1.3 × 10¹⁹ J, about **3,000 megatons of TNT** (1 Mt = 4.18 × 10¹⁵ J).

**STOP 5 · Exit ticket.** Momentum is conserved in the asteroid + Earth system, so Earth gains about 3.0 × 10¹⁵ kg·m/s. Divide by Earth's mass (6 × 10²⁴ kg): Δv ≈ **5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s**, essentially zero. Locally it's catastrophic because that same momentum (and the energy) is delivered to a **small area** in a **very short time**: a huge impulse, a huge local force, and enormous energy concentrated in one place.

**STOP 6 · Marzano on R2 and R3.**

## Formative checks
- The average force depends on the assumed collision duration.
- Momentum alone doesn't determine crater size or damage: students name the need for energy and material information.
