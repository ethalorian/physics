The equations of motion aren't memorized. They're measured.

Roll a cart down a ramp, record where it is and how fast it's going, and the four uniformly‑accelerated‑motion equations fall out of your own graphs. One experiment, four equations, no textbook required.

01 · The setup

A cart, a track, and a stack of books

The Go Direct Sensor Cart has an encoder in its wheels: it counts rotations and reports position and velocity many times a second. Nothing else is needed — no motion detector, no stopwatch. Tilt the track and gravity supplies a constant push along the ramp.

Equipment
  • Vernier Go Direct Sensor Cart
  • Vernier 1 m track with end stop
  • Books or blocks to raise one end
  • Meter stick to measure height h
  • Device running Vernier Graphical Analysis
Procedure
  1. Raise one end of the track. Measure h, the height of the track surface above the table at the 1 m mark.
  2. Connect the cart over Bluetooth and zero its position at the top of the track.
  3. Start collection, then release the cart from rest. Catch it before it hits the end stop hard.
  4. Select only the data after release and before the catch.
  5. Linear fit on velocity. Quadratic fit on position.
Watch out for
  • Negative velocity? The cart's positive direction points up‑ramp. Turn the cart around or flip the sign in the sensor settings.
  • Hand data. Points before release and after the catch aren't UAM — leave them out of every fit.
  • Steep ramps. Keep h under about 20 cm so the run lasts long enough to fit.

04 · Compare

Three measurements of a, one prediction

If the equations are right, every graph should report the same acceleration. And if gravity is the cause, that acceleration should match g·sinθ, where sinθ = h / (1.00 m).

The three graphs agree. Same cart, same run, three different fits — and one acceleration. That agreement is the evidence that all four equations describe the same motion.
All three fall a little short of g sinθ. Rolling friction and axle drag push back on the cart. The gap is roughly constant in m/s², so it matters most on shallow ramps. Try h = 2 cm, then h = 15 cm.
