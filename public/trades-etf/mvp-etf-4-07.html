P4 · 07 · Make an Operating Schedule and Energy Estimate

Use the actual model to demonstrate two room-use schedules, then calculate their energy with supplied lamp-power values.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Energy depends on both power and time. A useful schedule keeps necessary functions while avoiding unnecessary run time.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Working model, timer, two 3 × 5-in schedule cards and notebook. Use supplied calculation value 0.30 W per lamp; label it ASSUMED, not measured.

Finished means

  1. A model with a usable schedule card and verified state sequence.
  2. Two energy budgets, saved Wh and labeled assumptions.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Demonstrate schedule A for 60 seconds: S1 on and door open throughout; both lamps on.
  2. Demonstrate schedule B for 60 seconds: S1 on throughout; door open for the first 15 seconds, then shut. Record actual state timings.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Calculation schedule A represents both lamps on for 4 h. B represents L1 on 4 h and L2 on 1 h. The classroom minute is a compressed demonstration, not four hours of measured energy.
  2. Calculate both using the supplied 0.30-W value and compare Wh.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. Two 0.30-W lights each run for 4 h. Find total energy in Wh and kWh. Use a supplied classroom price of $0.25/kWh to estimate the energy-only cost.
  2. Two 0.30-W lights for 4 h: 2 × 0.30 × 4 = 2.4 Wh = 0.0024 kWh. At a supplied $0.25/kWh, cost is $0.0006.
  3. Sample electricity price is not a current tariff. Energy cost does not include battery purchase or equipment cost.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show both time bars, both energy totals and the percentage saving relative to A.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Write schedule A and B on separate cards with all switch/door instructions.
  2. Run A, then power OFF while preparing the reset. Run B and have another crew observe the changing lamp states.
  3. Calculate A: 2 × 0.30 × 4. Calculate B: 0.30 × 4 + 0.30 × 1.
  4. Find saved Wh and percent reduction relative to A. Calculate sample energy-only cost at $0.25/kWh.
  5. Attach schedule B beside the control and demonstrate it once more to a new operator.
  6. Store the model with holder OFF; do not leave it running for the imagined four-hour schedule.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Model demonstrates the stated switching sequence; calculations explicitly use assumed 0.30 W and represented hours.
  2. Energy cost is not battery purchase cost or a battery-runtime prediction.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
