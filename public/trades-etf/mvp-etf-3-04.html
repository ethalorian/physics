P3 · 04 · Add a Cargo Fence and Read the Assembly

Attach a three-sided cargo fence to the sled deck and make a complete measured assembly drawing.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Assembly drawings coordinate separate parts. You will locate a load-retaining feature without blocking actuator travel.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Working sled, three 2 × 1-in card strips, one paper cargo square, 18 in tape and a dry tray.

Finished means

  1. A fenced delivery sled with a complete assembly drawing and parts list.
  2. Notebook includes centerline/offset measurements and three cargo trials.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Assembly drawings coordinate separate parts. You will locate a load-retaining feature without blocking actuator travel.
  2. Required today: Attach a three-sided cargo fence to the sled deck and make a complete measured assembly drawing.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure deck width/depth and maximum rightward travel clearance.
  2. Mark the centerline 3 in from either long base edge. Measure barrel and saddle coordinates from the same lower-left datum.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Draw the base top view and a sled side detail. Include 12 × 6 base, x=3 nozzle, y=3 axis, 2 × 2 deck and 20-mm delivery distance.
  2. Add a parts list with two syringes, one 60-cm tube, base, two saddles, one sled and three fence strips.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A base is 3 in wide. Two mounting centers are 1 in apart and symmetric about its centerline. Locate both centers from the same left edge.
  2. On a 3-in-wide hull, the centerline is 1 1/2 in from each edge. Pipes 1 in apart lie 1/2 in on each side of center.
  3. Their centers lie 1 in and 2 in from the same edge.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show equal offsets from the centerline and a full loaded out-and-back movement.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut and fold the three fence strips along their centerlines.
  2. Tape one flat leg of each strip on the deck, upright leg along each of the two sides and front/right edge. Keep tape off the underside sliding surface.
  3. Leave the rear side open at the actuator tab. Do not bridge the moving deck to the stationary base.
  4. Place the cargo square inside the fence. Slowly cycle out and back three times.
  5. Measure any point where fence or tab touches a saddle. Reposition only the interfering tape/strip; do not change the delivery distance.
  6. Exchange drawings; partner identifies all parts on the actual assembly using the labels.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Cargo stays on deck for three complete cycles. Fence does not bind or reduce travel.
  2. Every listed part can be found on the drawing and object; moving and fixed parts are distinct.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: A fenced delivery sled with a complete assembly drawing and parts list.
  2. Notebook includes centerline/offset measurements and three cargo trials.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. Two mounting centers must be 1 1/2 in apart, symmetric about the centerline of a 4-in-wide base. Find both locations from the left edge. Identify which drawing provides their spacing.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
