Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A measured travel of 2.4 cm is compared with a 20-mm target. Convert to matching units and calculate the signed difference.
  2. 2.4 cm = 24 mm; 24 − 20 = 4 mm above a 20-mm target.
  3. Subtracting 2.4 from 20 without converting units gives a meaningless comparison.

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show before/after mount positions, matching units and the repeated travel check.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Inspect the deliberately shifted mount without operating it forcefully.
  2. Explain which dimension disagrees with the plan. Partner checks the datum, not the answer.
  3. Loosen saddle tape, restore nozzle and barrel to their original marks, then retape.
  4. Check sled tab square to deck and clear of any retained guide rails.
  5. Run three gentle 20-mm input strokes; record output travel and mount position each time.
  6. Each student repeats one location measurement and one stroke measurement without partner help.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Nozzle returns to marked x/y location within ±2 mm; output barrel remains fixed.
  2. Three strokes recorded; missed travel triggers inspection of bubbles/drag rather than more force.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
