ETF Project 1 · product design, prototyping and revision

P1 · 02 · Mock Up a Three-Bay Supply Tray

Build a folder-card tray with three side-by-side bays for erasers, binder clips and paper clips. Keep this version for comparison.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Storage fit depends on inside clearance, not just outside size. This prototype gives us a real fit problem to measure before using stronger material.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Folder card for the seven parts in drawing B; two erasers, three binder clips and six paper clips; 36 in of tape. B = base 8 × 6 in; F and R = two long walls 8 × 2 in; E1, E2, D1 and D2 = four short-wall blanks 6 × 2 in. Trim short walls to the measured gap between F and R.

Finished means

  1. One three-bay folder tray with seven labeled parts and the supplies in it.
  2. Notebook: top view, six clearance readings, retrieval results and one circled problem.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Storage fit depends on inside clearance, not just outside size. This prototype gives us a real fit problem to measure before using stronger material.
  2. Required today: Build a folder-card tray with three side-by-side bays for erasers, binder clips and paper clips. Keep this version for comparison.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure the three types of supplies at their widest point. Make a table: item | width | unit.
  2. Mark the base 8 × 6 in. From its left edge, mark lines at 2 5/8 and 5 1/4 in across the base; these locate the LEFT faces of D1 and D2.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Draw an 8 × 6-in top view. Label the front edge F and the left outer edge datum A.
  2. Copy the seven-part list. Assign left bay = erasers, middle = binder clips, right = paper clips. This is the required first arrangement.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. Three clear bays are each 2 1/2 in wide. Two internal dividers and two end walls are each 1/8 in thick. Find the width between the end walls and the overall outside width.
  2. 3 × 2 1/2 + 2 × 1/8 = 7 3/4 in interior; + 2 × 1/8 = 8 in outside.
  3. Only the two internal partitions belong in the interior-width sum.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to one divider and one end wall. Show six clear-bay readings and circle one snag on the top view.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut B, F and R to their labeled sizes. Cut E1/E2/D1/D2 initially as 6 × 2-in rectangles.
  2. Stand F and R on TOP of B, with their outside faces flush to its front and rear edges. Tape each wall to B inside and outside; keep walls upright.
  3. Set E1 between F and R at the left edge. Mark the actual inside gap on E1; trim to that mark so it fits without bending F/R. Use it to mark the other three short walls, then check each fit.
  4. Tape E1/E2 upright with outside faces flush to the left/right edges of B.
  5. Tape D1/D2 upright with their LEFT faces on the two base lines. Tape both ends to F/R and bottom edges to B.
  6. Put the assigned supplies in their bays. Partner removes and returns each type three times while the tray stays on the desk.
  7. Circle one snag on your notebook top view. Write one specific proposed repair: loose joint → reinforce; object catches divider → move that divider 1/4 in. Keep the prototype intact for day 3.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Each type fits its assigned bay and can be removed without lifting the tray. Record three attempts for each type.
  2. Measure each clear bay between wall faces at the front and rear. Record the six readings, even if they are unequal.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: One three-bay folder tray with seven labeled parts and the supplies in it.
  2. Notebook: top view, six clearance readings, retrieval results and one circled problem.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. Two clear bays each need 3 1/4 in. One internal partition is 1/8 in and two outer walls are each 1/8 in. Calculate outside width and identify the internal partition on your sketch.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
