P3 · 02 · Build the Syringe-Powered Delivery Sled

Mount the output syringe on a card base and attach a sliding card sled that moves out and back with the hand input.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

An actuator must be mounted so the moving part can do useful work. This device delivers a light paper load to a marked position without touching the sled.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Prepared water pair, card stock for the base, two saddle strips, one sled blank, 30 in tape and one 1 × 1-in paper cargo square. 12 × 6-in base; output nozzle at x = 3 in on centerline y = 3 in; two 4 × 1-in card saddle strips; sled blank 3 × 2 in, folded 1 in from one short end.

Finished means

  1. One water-powered sliding delivery sled with start/delivery marks and paper cargo.
  2. Dimensioned assembly sketch and three travel readings.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Mark base datum A at lower-left; draw centerline y=3 in. Mark nozzle location x=3 in.
  2. With output at 5 mL, check there is room to its right for the 2-in sled deck plus at least 20 mm travel before the base edge. Teacher checks fit before taping.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. In an ideal liquid system, the input area is 1 cm² and the output area is 2 cm². The input piston moves 4 cm. Predict output travel.
  2. A_in = 1 cm²; A_out = 2 cm²; input 4 cm: output = (1 × 4)/2 = 2 cm.
  3. Ideal output force would double, but do not claim you measured a force ratio without force measurements.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to what must stay still and what must move. Demonstrate travel from the same starting mark.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut base to 12 × 6 in. Cut two 4 × 1-in saddle strips and sled blank 3 × 2 in.
  2. Fold sled across its 2-in width at 1 in from a short end. Stand the 1-in tab upright; the remaining 2 × 2-in deck lies on the base.
  3. Lay output on centerline, nozzle at x=3 in and pointing left. Keep its 5-mL start position.
  4. Curve each saddle over the fixed barrel, then crease its two ends down onto the base. Tape those ends firmly to the base. Do not tape or pinch the moving plunger.
  5. Put the sled tab against the right-facing flat thumb pad, deck extending right. Tape tab to pad on both accessible sides; the deck must slide flat rather than hang above the base.
  6. Route the 60-cm tube in a broad loop to input off the left side. Tape the tube loosely to the base near the nozzle for strain relief; never flatten the tube.
  7. Move input slowly 20 mm. Sled should travel right. Pull input back to return the sled; do not reset it by hand.
  8. Draw START at the sled’s front/right edge in the reset position and a delivery line 20 mm to its right. Put the paper cargo square on the deck.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Do three out-and-back cycles. The input hand is the only drive; output barrel stays fixed and sled clears the base edge.
  2. Record actual sled travel each time. Classroom acceptance for equal-syringe travel is 20 mm ±2 mm, after any bubble/mount repair; failures remain recorded.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
