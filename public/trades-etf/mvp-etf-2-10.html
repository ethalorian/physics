P2 · 10 · Clinic: Move the Door and Coordinate Both Views

Make a replacement south wall with the door shifted to a 3-ft west offset, then update both drawings.

Why this job?

A change order must reach every affected drawing and part. Today’s required change is precise: west offset becomes 3 ft; door size stays 3 × 7 ft.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Room model, fresh 8 × 4-in south wall, 18 in tape and spare drawing paper.

Finished means

  1. A physically relocated door and two coordinated revised views.
  2. Notebook change record and original wall retained.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: A change order must reach every affected drawing and part. Today’s required change is precise: west offset becomes 3 ft; door size stays 3 × 7 ft.
  2. Required today: Make a replacement south wall with the door shifted to a 3-ft west offset, then update both drawings.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure and record the old door offset before removal.
  2. Mark the new jambs and 3 1/2-in height from the same west/bottom datums on the replacement wall.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Cross out the old 2-ft offset with one line on plan and south elevation; write 3 ft, C-01 and date.
  2. Calculate model movement and mark both old and new jamb locations on a comparison sketch.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. At 1/2 in = 1 ft, a door’s full-size offset changes from 2 ft to 3 ft. How far must the door move in the model?
  2. At 1/2 in per ft, changing 2 ft to 3 ft moves the opening by 1/2 in in the model.
  3. The designer must decide the intended offset before either view is corrected.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show the original and revised offsets in both views, then demonstrate the working route.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut top and right jamb of the new door; fold left jamb as before.
  2. Remove the old south wall by peeling its tape; retain it as revision B.
  3. Fit the new south wall with outer face flush to base. Tape to base and end walls while leaving the door free.
  4. Move a table token only if the new door arc interferes; update that location on the plan.
  5. Check new door offset, size, swing and token route.
  6. Exchange views with a partner and have them locate the new opening without verbal help.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. New left jamb is 1 1/2 in from west datum within ±1/16; opening size is unchanged.
  2. Plan, elevation and physical wall all show C-01.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: A physically relocated door and two coordinated revised views.
  2. Notebook change record and original wall retained.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.

  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.
