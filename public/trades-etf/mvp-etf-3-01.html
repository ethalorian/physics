P3 · 01 · Compare Air and Water Syringe Motion

Operate air and water syringe pairs; attach a paper indicator and measure actual input/output travel.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Hydraulic technicians distinguish fluid behavior from mechanical binding. We will use measured motion, not how “strong” a system feels.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. One matching 20-mL syringe pair, 60-cm tube, water tray and towel, two 1/4 × 2-in paper pointer strips and removable tape. Teacher prepares the water fill after the air trial.

Finished means

  1. A working water-filled pair, clearly labeled input/output.
  2. Six measured trial rows and a connection sketch.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Set input to 15 mL and output to 5 mL before connecting the air-filled tube.
  2. Tape a paper pointer to each thumb pad. Lay a ruler beside each barrel, parallel to its axis, and mark the pointer starting position.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Draw input → tube → output. Label air trial pneumatic and water trial hydraulic.
  2. Make six rows: fluid | trial | input travel mm | output travel mm | bubbles/response. Target hand stroke is 20 mm.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. Two ideal liquid-filled syringes have equal piston areas. The input piston moves 20 mm. Use displaced volume to predict output travel.
  2. Equal piston areas predict equal travel: A × 20 mm = A × d, so d = 20 mm.
  3. This model fits an ideal liquid system; trapped air, leaks and friction can change actual motion.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show all three output readings for each fluid. Point to the fixed barrels and moving plungers.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Place both syringes flat in the dry tray; partners hold BARRELS, not output plungers.
  2. Move input forward slowly by 20 mm while partner reads output travel. Stop if resistance rises sharply; never force.
  3. Pull input back to its original mark. Repeat three trials, swapping operator and reader.
  4. Take the pair to the teacher for water filling at the same 15/5-mL starting positions. The tube and both tips must be water-filled, with visible bubbles removed.
  5. Wipe outside dry. Repeat the same three 20-mm strokes and resets with water.
  6. Check tip joints for drops and circle any delayed or unequal motion. Save the water-filled pair capped within its tray.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Input/output travel comes from pointer start/end positions in mm. Both students take an actual reading.
  2. Equal-bore ideal liquid prediction is equal travel; record actual results even when they differ.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
