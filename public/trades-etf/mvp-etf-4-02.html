P4 · 02 · Make the Door Trigger Reliable

Improve the same detector with a tongue guide and verify ten repeatable door-open signals.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Reliability is repeatable operation under a stated procedure. You will fix alignment and distinguish circuit connectivity from physical placement.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Detector, one 3 × 1-in card strip, 12 in tape and the existing 1 × 3-in tongue.

Finished means

  1. A detector with guided tongue and two distinct drawings.
  2. Ten-trial reliability record before/after any adjustment.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  2. Measure the clothespin jaw location from base datum and add it to the physical layout.

  2. Make a ten-row table: tongue inserted OFF | door open ON | reset success.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A trigger succeeds 8 times in 10 trials. Find its success percentage. Compare it with 10 successes in 10 trials, and state what the test cannot prove.
  2. Eight successful triggers in ten trials = 80%; ten in ten = 100% in that test.
  3. Ten successes do not prove permanent reliability under all conditions.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show ten OFF/ON pairs and identify any intermittent failure.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Switch holder OFF. Wrap the 3 × 1-in strip loosely around the tongue to make a sleeve; leave about 1/16 in sliding clearance.
  2. Tape the sleeve ends to the fixed base/wall beside the clothespin, keeping it clear of jaws and foil.
  3. Slide tongue through the sleeve into the open clothespin, then release jaws and close door.
  4. Teacher checks connections remain unchanged. Turn holder ON and perform ten door-open/manual-reset cycles.
  5. Swap operator after five. Record each state, not only an overall percentage.
  6. If it catches, widen the sleeve or realign clothespin once. Repeat the full ten cycles and label the revision.
  7. Power OFF and label final jaw/guide locations on the layout.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Target ten correct OFF/ON pairs with no hidden contact assistance; record any failure.
  2. Moving the clothespin changes layout coordinates but need not change the schematic connections.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
