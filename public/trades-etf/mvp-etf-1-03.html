P1 · 03 · Measure and Cut the Durable Tray Parts

Make the seven fitted parts for a stronger copy of the same three-bay tray; dry-fit them before assembly.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Fabricators use a cut list to avoid wasting stock. You will transfer dimensions and account for the actual wall thickness.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Previous prototype, clean thin packaging card totaling at least 130 in² of usable area per crew, and 12 in of temporary tape. B = base 8 × 6 in; F and R = two long walls 8 × 2 in; E1, E2, D1 and D2 = four short-wall blanks 6 × 2 in. Trim short walls to the measured gap between F and R.

Finished means

  1. Seven labeled durable parts that dry-fit the tray plan.
  2. Notebook: stock layout, complete final cut list and two inspection readings per student.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Fabricators use a cut list to avoid wasting stock. You will transfer dimensions and account for the actual wall thickness.

  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure usable stock. Lay out 8 × 6 base, two 8 × 2 walls and four 6 × 2 blanks; each student marks at least one part.
  2. Mark D1/D2 positions at 2 5/8 and 5 1/4 in from the base’s left edge. Check both endpoints before drawing each line.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Make a cut-list table: part | quantity | blank length | height/depth | final fitted length. Enter B×1, F/R×2, E/D×4.
  2. Draw the rectangles on available stock with no overlaps. Shared scissors cuts need no added saw kerf. Preserve long straight offcuts.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. Four rectangular parts are each 4 × 3 in. A sheet measures 9 × 12 in. Calculate the area used and describe a layout that proves all four parts fit.
  2. Each 4 × 3-in piece has 12 in² area; four use 48 in². Two rows of two fit in an 8 × 6-in rectangle.
  3. A 9 × 12-in sheet can contain this arrangement. Area alone would not prove a different set of shapes fits.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show the scrap gauge, the fitted wall and your intended/actual/difference record.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut B, F and R first. Label each part on the face that will be outside.
  2. Temporarily tape F/R upright on B with outer faces flush to the base edges.
  3. Use a narrow scrap strip to capture the inside gap: hold one end against F, mark where it meets the inside face of R, then measure that length.
  4. Transfer that gap to all four short-wall blanks. Mark a waste X on the portion to remove.
  5. Partner checks the first marked short wall; trim it and test it between F/R without bending either wall.
  6. Trim the remaining three to the same checked length. Label E1/E2/D1/D2.
  7. Dry-fit all seven pieces in their specified positions. Do not permanently tape partitions today. Bundle small parts in the base with the cut list.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Each short wall fits between F/R without force and with no end gap greater than 1/8 in.
  2. Check one 8-in part and one fitted part independently. Record intended, actual and signed difference; ±1/8 in is the classroom cutting tolerance.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: Seven labeled durable parts that dry-fit the tray plan.
  2. Notebook: stock layout, complete final cut list and two inspection readings per student.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. A 12 × 9-in sheet supplies six 4 × 3-in pieces. Show an arrangement, calculate used area and remaining area, and explain why the layout matters.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
