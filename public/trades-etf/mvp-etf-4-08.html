Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A system has four required switch states. Each is tested three times and 11 checks pass. How many checks were required? Does the result establish that every required check passed?
  2. Four required switch states × three repeats = 12 checks. Eleven passes means one condition still needs repair.
  3. Do not hide a failed control state inside an overall average.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to the break and show the original failed state plus the full retest.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Teacher prepares the one stated open connection with power OFF.
  2. Power ON briefly and observe four states; record symptoms without moving clips.
  3. Power OFF. Trace L2’s entire path and point to the missing return link on the drawing.
  4. Reconnect L2-B to supply −; teacher checks exposed clips are separated.
  5. Power ON and repeat all four S1/door states three times. Record each of twelve checks.
  6. Give a one-minute handover with door-reset and power-off instructions. Each student performs an individual schematic/measurement/math check.
  7. Power OFF and inventory lamps, switches, leads and cells.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. L1 remains independent and L2 responds to door after repair; all twelve checks documented.
  2. Fault report names the open return and evidence rather than “the battery was bad” without a test.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
