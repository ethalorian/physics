P2 · 01 · Build and Load-Test Two Paper Towers

Build two 11-in paper columns supporting a 6 × 6-in platform; add base ties and repeat the same capped load test.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Builders need a continuous load path and stable supports. A capped test shows what the model carried under specified conditions.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Two letter sheets for two 6 1/2 × 11-in column blanks, two 6 × 1-in offcut ties, one 6 × 6-in card platform, one small paper cup, ten identical large paper clips and 36 in of tape.

Finished means

  1. One tower with two triangular columns and removable base ties.
  2. Two load records and an annotated load-path sketch.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Builders need a continuous load path and stable supports. A capped test shows what the model carried under specified conditions.
  2. Required today: Build two 11-in paper columns supporting a 6 × 6-in platform; add base ties and repeat the same capped load test.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Mark both blanks from the same long edge at 2/4/6 in; measure height 11 in.
  2. Draw a 6 × 6-in footprint on scrap. Keep both columns and platform inside it.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Sketch the side and top views; show the two triangular column footprints under the left/right sides of the platform.
  2. Prepare a load table with rows 0 through 10 clips for version A and B. The cup/platform are the same in both tests.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. The first tower holds 4 identical test objects. A revised tower holds 7. Calculate the percentage increase using the first tower as the baseline.
  2. Version 1 holds 4 identical objects; version 2 holds 7: (7 − 4)/4 × 100 = 75% more.
  3. Object count is a comparative load measure, not force in newtons. A capped test gives a lower bound on capacity.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show each last load held for five seconds and identify the first failure location—or the test cap.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut, fold and tape each blank into a triangular tube with three 2-in faces. Keep both ends level.
  2. Stand the columns on the marked footprint, one under each side of the platform. Place the 6 × 6-in platform across them; tape its contacts lightly.
  3. Put the empty cup at the platform center. If it cannot support the cup, repair the column seams before recording a load trial.
  4. Add one paper clip every five seconds up to ten. Stop immediately if a column tips, folds or the platform falls; do not catch a falling load with the model.
  5. Unload. Cut two 6 × 1-in ties from offcuts. Tape one tie across the front column bases and one across the rear bases to resist spreading; do not tape the tower to the table.
  6. Repeat the same 0–10-clip test with the same cup, platform and placement. Record the cap if both versions hold ten.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Freestanding footprint no larger than 6 × 6 in; height 11 in ±1/4.
  2. Record last load held for five seconds and failure location. Ten clips means “held the test cap,” not “maximum strength is ten.”

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
