P2 · 08 · Seal One Insulation Gap and Retest

Improve the insulated cup’s top-edge fit, repeat the comparison, and add a removable insulation panel to the room.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

A redesign needs one identified change and comparable test conditions. You will change the top-edge seal while holding the rest of the test constant.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Previous cup enclosure and wall sample, fresh equal ice masses, identical control materials, same balance/test equipment, one 4 × 1-in paper strip and two tape loops.

Finished means

  1. Revised cup enclosure and model with removable wall-layer panel.
  2. Before/after sections and two control-referenced melt comparisons.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure the largest gap between insulation wrap and lid; record to the ruler’s nearest division.
  2. Measure the north wall panel area to confirm the 4 × 4-in sample will fit without covering a door or roof locator.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Draw revision C with the added collar. Keep the original section visible; circle only the new piece.
  2. Copy original melt values and conditions into a before/after table. Each new run also needs its own control cup.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. In a supplied structural example, measured deflection falls from 6 mm to 4 mm. Calculate the percentage reduction relative to the first measurement.
  2. Deflection falls from 6 mm to 4 mm: (6 − 4)/6 × 100 ≈ 33.3% reduction.
  3. Report measurement resolution; a small difference may not be meaningful enough to justify a strong claim.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to the single change. Show the original and revised records with their own controls.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Dry the enclosure completely. Replace damaged paper with the same number of layers.
  2. Make a 1-in-high collar of paper, length = measured cup circumference + 1 in; join strips if required. Fit it around the top of the wrap to bridge the lid-edge gap; do not add layers elsewhere.
  3. Repeat the equal-ice, matching-control test for the same agreed interval and 30-second drainage used on day 7.
  4. During the wait, attach the 4 × 4-in wall sample inside the north wall using two removable tape loops.
  5. Lift the roof, remove/replace the panel and swing the door 90 degrees. Adjust loops if access is blocked.
  6. Compare each insulated result with its own control. If melt is greater after revision, keep the data and write that the change did not improve this trial.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. One intentional thermal change: top collar. Same mass, interval, lids, location and procedure.
  2. Model panel is removable; door and roof access still work.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
