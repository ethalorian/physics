Why this job?

A machine handover needs operating instructions, repeatability evidence and clear limits. An average alone cannot certify every delivery.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Final sled, baseline/revision records, assembly drawing and same paper cargo.

Finished means

  1. A working delivery machine, operator card and accurate final assembly plan.
  2. Individual math, measurement and plan-reading evidence plus the recorded redesign.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: A machine handover needs operating instructions, repeatability evidence and clear limits. An average alone cannot certify every delivery.
  2. Required today: Hand over a working syringe sled and explain its motion, measured travel and tested revision.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Individually measure one actual 20-mm delivery stroke and the nozzle’s x position from datum A.
  2. Review each measured travel; flag a stroke outside 20 ±2 mm even if the mean looks acceptable.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Prepare a four-line operator card: load cargo; reset to START; move input until DELIVERY; pull input back to START. Add “stop if resistance rises.”
  2. Choose one baseline and one revision result from actual notebook records, with units.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A sled must travel at least 20 mm on every trial. It travels 22, 21 and 19 mm. Does it meet the requirement? Explain using the individual results.
  2. A gate must move at least 20 mm. Three trials of 22, 21 and 19 mm include one failure.
  3. An average above 20 mm does not prove every trial meets an every-cycle requirement.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show every actual travel reading, not only the average. Point to the criterion used.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Exchange machines and operator cards. New operator follows the card without coaching.
  2. Perform three complete deliveries, recording each travel and any unclear instruction.
  3. Return the machine. Owners fix one instruction or physical snag and repeat three deliveries.
  4. Give a one-minute demonstration identifying how input motion travels through water to the output.
  5. Each student reads a supplied assembly location, takes a tape/ruler reading and explains one rate calculation to the teacher.
  6. Return plungers to reset marks and store connected pair in a tray; teacher drains/stores water systems according to the kit routine after the project.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. All three deliveries meet the classroom stroke check or remaining faults are named.
  2. Claim only what was tested: a low-load water-powered sled, not a heated boat or measured force multiplier.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: A working delivery machine, operator card and accurate final assembly plan.
  2. Individual math, measurement and plan-reading evidence plus the recorded redesign.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. A 20-mm minimum travel requirement gives trials 20, 23 and 18 mm. Does the machine meet an every-cycle requirement? Calculate the mean and explain its limitation.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
