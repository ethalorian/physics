P2 · 07 · Build and Test an Insulated Cup Enclosure

Make a two-layer paper enclosure for one ice cup and compare it with an identical uninsulated control cup.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Envelope performance requires a fair comparison. You will keep starting ice, time and handling the same while changing the insulation.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Two identical dry 240–250-mL plastic cups, two identical paper/card lids, two letter-paper sheets, tape, tray, timer, sieve, catch cup, 1-g balance and 120 g ice per crew (60 g for each cup). Shared balance station is acceptable.

Finished means

  1. One insulated cup enclosure and one 4 × 4-in wall-layer sample.
  2. Actual control/test melt results, a section sketch and a supported comparison.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure cup circumference with a paper strip, marking one full wrap; measure cup height with ruler.
  2. At the balance station tare each dry cup and add 60 g ice to each. Start both cups together.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Draw a section: cup, two paper wrap layers and 1/2-in crumpled-paper bottom pad. Label the control’s matching lid.
  2. Prepare table: cup | starting ice g | elapsed min | collected melt g | observations. Test interval is 20 min.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A control and an insulated cup each start with 60 g of ice. The control melts 12 g and the insulated cup melts 6 g. Find each melted percentage and the percentage reduction in melt relative to the control.
  2. 12 g melted from 60 g = 20%; 6 g from 60 g = 10%. Melt reduction = (12 − 6)/12 × 100 = 50%.
  3. The enclosure halved melt in this trial; it did not establish a material R-value.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show the matched starting conditions and both melt results, including the scale’s 1-g resolution.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut two paper strips each cup-height tall and circumference + 1 in long. Tape them end-to-end if needed to reach that measured length.
  2. Wrap the test cup twice, one strip at a time, loosely enough to leave small air spaces; tape only the seams. Keep paper dry and outside the cup.
  3. Make a 1/2-in-thick lightly crumpled pad from remaining paper and place it under the test cup. Control sits directly on the same tray.
  4. Add the equal ice masses, put matching lids on both and start a 20-minute timer. Keep them side-by-side away from sun/vents.
  5. During the wait, make a 4 × 4-in two-layer wall sample for the model room and draw its layers; do not disturb cups.
  6. At 20 minutes drain each cup through the same sieve into the tared catch cup for 30 seconds. Weigh the collected melt, drying/taring the catch cup between samples.
  7. Calculate control melt minus insulated melt. Calculate percent reduction only if control melt is greater than zero; keep all readings even if insulation did worse.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Both start with 60 g ice, have the same lid and run for the same 20 minutes; record any deviation.
  2. Readings at/below the 1-g instrument resolution are inconclusive. Do not replace them with the worked example or claim an R-value.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
