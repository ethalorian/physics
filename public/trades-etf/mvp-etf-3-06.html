Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A delivery machine completes 5 full cycles in 25 s. Calculate its average rate in cycles per second.
  2. 5 complete cycles in 25 s = 0.20 cycles/s.
  3. A cycle includes travel from START to DELIVERY and back to START; one outward stroke is not a cycle.

Stop & show

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Put cargo on deck. Operator holds input; timer watches the sled edge and calls “ready.”
  2. Timer says “go” and starts clock as operator begins. Count a cycle only when sled reaches DELIVERY and returns to START.
  3. Stop timer at the fifth return. If a cycle misses either mark, label trial invalid and record the missed endpoint.
  4. Reset and repeat until there are three valid trials; retain invalid trials with reasons.
  5. Rotate roles so each student operates and measures. Use gentle motion and the same cargo throughout.
  6. Calculate 5 ÷ elapsed seconds for each trial. Calculate mean total time from the three valid trials.
  7. Inspect the sled without modifying it. Mark the two side-fence top edges as tomorrow’s guide-rail reference.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Three valid five-cycle trials; every endpoint reached. No direct pushing of output.
  2. All rates use cycles/s, not cm/s. Record observed spread rather than reporting a single fastest run.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
