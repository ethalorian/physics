P2 · 03 · Survey a Doorway and Lay Out the Community Room

Measure a real doorway, then make the supplied community-room floor plan. Keep the two sets of dimensions separate.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Surveyors record existing conditions; designers read proposed conditions. Mixing them can put an opening in the wrong place.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Two sheets of paper and access to one teacher-designated classroom wall/doorway; two 1 × 2-in card table tokens and one 1/2 × 1/2-in person token.

Finished means

  1. One measured survey sketch and one proposed floor layout with two table tokens and a clear route.
  2. Notebook labels distinguish measured school values from supplied design values.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Surveyors record existing conditions; designers read proposed conditions. Mixing them can put an opening in the wrong place.
  2. Required today: Measure a real doorway, then make the supplied community-room floor plan. Keep the two sets of dimensions separate.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. At the designated doorway, identify the classroom-side left wall corner as survey datum. Measure corner-to-near-jamb and opening width to nearest 1/4 in.
  2. Measure twice, with roles swapped. Record both readings in feet/inches AND total inches. Keep the passage clear.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Title one sketch EXISTING DOOR SURVEY and show the datum and two actual measurements.
  2. On a second sheet draw the PROPOSED ROOM at 1/2 in = 1 ft: 8 × 6 in, north at top, door opening along bottom from x=1 to 2 1/2 in.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A surveyed length is 12 ft 6 in. Convert the complete length to inches and show both parts of the conversion.
  2. 12 ft 6 in = 12 × 12 + 6 = 150 in.
  3. 6 in is half a foot, not 0.6 ft.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Show one measured school dimension and one supplied design dimension. Trace a clear route.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Mark the model floor rectangle and door jambs from the southwest datum.
  2. Cut two 1 × 2-in table tokens and label them 2 × 4-ft tables.
  3. Put both tables against the north edge, with their 2-in sides east-west. Leave 1 in between them.
  4. Cut a 1/2-in square person token. Move it from the south entrance to each table without crossing a table.
  5. Trace a clear route on the floor plan. If blocked, move only the eastern table toward the east wall and repeat.
  6. Attach tokens with small tape loops so they remain movable. Store the floor plan for the next lesson.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. A partner locates the surveyed opening from written dimensions alone.
  2. The proposed plan remains exactly 8 × 6 in with the specified door position. The token can reach both tables; this is a model-use test, not an accessibility certification.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: One measured survey sketch and one proposed floor layout with two table tokens and a clear route.
  2. Notebook labels distinguish measured school values from supplied design values.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. Convert a 9 ft 8-in surveyed length to inches. On drawing E, how far from the southwest corner is the far edge of the 3-ft door?
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
