P1 · 01 · Build a Stand for an Instruction Card

Make one triangular stand for a 3 × 5-in card, then improve its card-supporting lip. The 5-in card edge rests horizontally on the 4-in-wide stand.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

A technician must turn a flat pattern into a useful object. Today you will read a cut/fold plan, measure fractions from one datum and check whether the product works.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Two folder-card rectangles at least 10 1/2 × 4 in, one 4 × 1-in lip blank, one 4 × 1 1/2-in replacement lip blank, one paper/card rectangle 3 × 5 in, and 24 in of tape. Keep unused stock for a remake.

Finished means

  1. One labeled triangular stand with a 3 × 5-in card; the spare lip beside it.
  2. Notebook: labeled side view, actual F/B lengths, and a two-row table: lip depth | trial 1 | trial 2 | trial 3. Save the individual transfer answer in the app.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: A technician must turn a flat pattern into a useful object. Today you will read a cut/fold plan, measure fractions from one datum and check whether the product works.
  2. Required today: Make one triangular stand for a 3 × 5-in card, then improve its card-supporting lip. The 5-in card edge rests horizontally on the 4-in-wide stand.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Cut the test card to 3 × 5 in. Mark its 5-in edge BOTTOM. The load today is this card.
  2. On the body stock, choose the left 4-in edge as datum A. Mark 0, 3, 6, 10 and 10 1/2 in along BOTH long edges, always measuring from A.
  3. Join matching marks with straight lines. Check overall width 4 in and length 10 1/2 in with your partner; then swap who reads the tape.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Label F between 0–3 in, R between 3–6, B between 6–10, and T between 10–10 1/2. Do not cut the three inside lines.
  2. Sketch the finished triangular side view in your notebook: two 3-in sloping faces and a 4-in base. Add the 1/2-in overlap tab on a separate detail.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A stand blank is 10 1/2 in long, including a 1/2-in joining tab. How much length forms the three sides? Check against sides of 3 in, 3 in and 4 in.
  2. 10 1/2 − 1/2 = 10 in; 3 + 3 + 4 = 10 in.
  3. The joining tab overlaps; it is not a fourth side of the triangular stand.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Hold up the stand. Point to F, B and the overlapping tab. Show both sets of three trials.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Cut the OUTSIDE of the 10 1/2 × 4-in blank. Write your crew name on B.
  2. Fold at 3, 6 and 10 in, all toward the same side of the card. Press the creases against a ruler; do not cut or score with a blade.
  3. Place B flat on the desk. Bring the free edge of F to the fold between B and T. The 3-in faces meet at the top; the cross-section is a triangle.
  4. Put T inside F, overlapping it by 1/2 in. Tape the seam across the full 4-in width. The tab is a joint, not a fourth side.
  5. Cut L to 4 × 1 in. Draw and fold a line along its length, making two 4 × 1/2-in halves.
  6. Tape one half of L to F at the bottom of the front face, with the lip fold at the front/base corner. Leave the other half projecting forward as a shelf; adjust that shelf approximately level with the tabletop.
  7. Rest the card’s BOTTOM edge on L and lean the card back against F. Center it: about 1/2 in of card extends beyond each side of the stand.
  8. Run the first three-trial test. Then cut a second lip 4 × 1 1/2 in; fold 1/2 in from one long edge. Replace L, attaching the 1/2-in half to F so the shelf is now 1 in deep. Label the new lip revision B.
  9. Repeat the identical test. Keep the better lip on the stand; retain the removed lip with your notebook. If the body failed, remake it to the original dimensions before comparing lips.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Trial: put the stand on a level desk, place the card, remove both hands and time 10 seconds. Pass only if both stand and card stay upright. Remove the card; repeat for three trials.
  2. Do three trials for the 1/2-in lip, then three for the 1-in lip. Record each pass/fail; a tie is a valid result.
  3. Measure F and B along their fold-to-fold edges. Classroom layout check: 3 in and 4 in, each within ±1/8 in. Correct a wrong fold even if the card happens to stand.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: One labeled triangular stand with a 3 × 5-in card; the spare lip beside it.
  2. Notebook: labeled side view, actual F/B lengths, and a two-row table: lip depth | trial 1 | trial 2 | trial 3. Save the individual transfer answer in the app.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. A revised stand uses two 3 1/4-in faces, a 4-in base and a 1/2-in joining tab. Find its blank length. Explain where you would measure the second fold from datum A. Cite your notebook and one test result.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
