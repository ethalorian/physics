P3 · 03 · Measure Flow Through Two Tube Routes

At the prepared gravity station, time 15 mL of water through straight and curved routes; then secure a smooth tube route on the sled.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Pipe work requires quantities and rates as well as a route drawing. A fair comparison controls starting head, tube and outlet height.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Existing sled. Shared station: 20-mL syringe barrel with plunger removed, stand/clamp, the same 60-cm tube for both routes, catch cup, water tray, ruler and stopwatch.

Finished means

  1. A secured tube route on the sled and two measured route drawings.
  2. Six actual rates with conditions and a comparison limit.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Measure the reservoir 5-mL-line-to-outlet vertical drop as 10 cm and secure outlet at that height.
  2. Measure the same tube’s actual length 60 cm. Record bore only if the kit states it; do not guess from outside diameter.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Sketch route A as one broad U and B as a broad S, with identical inlet/outlet locations and no uphill crest trapping air.
  2. Make table: route | trial | volume 15 mL | time s | rate mL/s. Three trials for each route.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A collection trial produces 180 mL in 15 s. Find the average flow rate. At that average rate, how long would collecting 240 mL take?
  2. 180 mL in 15 s = 12 mL/s. At that average rate, 240 mL would take 20 s.
  3. A falling reservoir changes pressure head; the reported value is the trial’s average.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to the two timing marks and outlet height. Show volume, time and flow rate for each trial.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Teacher clamps the upright barrel and secures tube outlet over a cup in the tray.
  2. Fill tube completely with water first. Pinch the tube gently near its outlet while filling reservoir above the 20-mL mark.
  3. Release. Start the timer as the level crosses 20 mL; stop at 5 mL. Do not touch the reservoir during flow.
  4. Refill and repeat twice. Rotate reader/timer/recorder; pairs share the recording role.
  5. Rearrange only the middle of the tube into route B, maintaining length and both endpoint heights. Repeat three trials.
  6. Calculate 15 ÷ time for each valid trial. A tied result is acceptable; hand timing limits small comparisons.
  7. Reconnect the teacher-checked water pair on the sled. Route its tube in a broad untwisted loop and tape a loose guide to keep it away from the moving sled.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Three valid timed trials per route; same marks, tube and outlet height. Mark spills or bubbles invalid and repeat.
  2. The sled still performs three strokes after reconnection. Gravity-flow results do not directly predict syringe-machine speed under hand pressure.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
