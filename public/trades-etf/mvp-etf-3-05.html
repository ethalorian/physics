P3 · 05 · Find and Repair a Loose Actuator Mount

Diagnose a deliberately loose saddle, repair it, and prove the sled repeats three accurate deliveries.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Repair begins with an observed fault, a predicted cause and a controlled retest. Replacing random parts can hide the original problem.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Working sled, cargo square, ruler, towel and 12 in replacement tape. Teacher prepares one loose saddle end with power supplied only by hand input at rest.

Finished means

  1. A repaired, stable sled with accessible connections.
  2. Notebook contains symptom, evidence, a named repair and retest readings.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Draw a pencil witness line at the output barrel’s original position on the base.
  2. Measure barrel shift and sled travel during one gentle 20-mm input stroke. Stop if the mount releases.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Make a fault table: symptom | suspected part | evidence | repair | retest.
  2. Circle the suspect saddle on the assembly drawing before touching its tape.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A system starts with 200 mL. The receiver contains 175 mL and 20 mL remains in the route. Find the volume that is still unaccounted for.
  2. Start 200 mL; receiver 175 mL; liquid retained 20 mL: unaccounted volume = 5 mL.
  3. Do not call all 25 mL missing from the receiver a leak.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Point to the fault, the witness mark and the measurement that changed after repair.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Teacher peels one end of the rear saddle from the base; do not disconnect or obstruct the tube.
  2. Operate one gentle stroke while partner watches the barrel witness line. Record the symptom.
  3. Return input to its start; keep hands off the moving sled. Explain the suspected mount to your partner.
  4. Remove loose tape. Reposition barrel on centerline and nozzle datum, then retape the saddle end firmly.
  5. Dry the tray and outside connections with a towel. Inspect for fresh drops during three slow cycles; do not treat old tray water as new leakage.
  6. Run three cargo deliveries from the same start mark and measure actual travel each time.
  7. Add the repair and three results to the fault table.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Barrel stays on its witness line and each travel is 20 mm ±2 mm. Record any remaining failure.
  2. A repair is complete only after the same motion test repeats without hands holding the output.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.
