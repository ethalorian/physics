P2 · 05 · Test a Wall Frame and Add a Diagonal

Build a separate 6 × 8-in joint-center frame, test its racking and add a diagonal brace; then reinforce the model room’s corners.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

A diagonal changes how a rectangular frame resists shape change. You will connect a measured test with a bracing decision.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Two 9 × 1-in and two 7 × 1-in card strips, one 11 × 1-in brace, four 2 × 2-in corner squares, string, cup with ten large paper clips, two books and the model room.

Finished means

  1. A braced test frame and four reinforced corners on the room model.
  2. Two diagonals and before/after racking data with a labeled frame sketch.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: A diagonal changes how a rectangular frame resists shape change. You will connect a measured test with a bracing decision.
  2. Required today: Build a separate 6 × 8-in joint-center frame, test its racking and add a diagonal brace; then reinforce the model room’s corners.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Mark each strip centerline and points 1/2 in from its ends. Mark brace attachment centers 1/2 and 10 1/2 in from one end.
  2. Measure both frame diagonals between joint centers before the test; a square 6 × 8-in rectangle has two 10-in diagonals.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Sketch the four joint centers as a 6 × 8-in rectangle. Draw the brace from bottom-left to top-right.
  2. Make table: unbraced / braced | starting top-right height | loaded height | downward movement, in mm.

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. A rectangular frame has perpendicular sides of 6 in and 8 in. Calculate the distance between opposite corners. State the geometric condition needed for your calculation.
  2. For a 6 × 8-in rectangle: d² = 6² + 8² = 100; d = 10 in.
  3. This uses a right angle. Check the frame; do not apply it to an arbitrary four-sided shape.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Trace the brace between joint centers. Show both diagonals and before/after movement.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Arrange the two long strips horizontally and short strips vertically with center marks overlapping. Tape each overlap on both faces; keep joints flexible enough to rack.
  2. Stand the frame vertical. Sandwich its left upright between two upright books and tape it to the outer book face so both left joint centers stay fixed. Teacher checks the books are stable.
  3. Tie the loaded cup to the TOP-RIGHT joint with a short string. Keep the cup close to the tabletop; no one holds the right side.
  4. Measure the top-right joint height from the tabletop before loading and after five seconds of load. Remove the cup.
  5. Return the frame to its original rectangular shape. Tape the diagonal between bottom-left and top-right centers; use the 11-in strip’s 1/2-in end allowances.
  6. Repeat with the identical cup/clip load and fixed left side.
  7. Fold each 2 × 2-in square into an angle bracket. Tape one inside each corner of the model room without blocking its door; label this model revision B.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. Compare vertical movement at the same joint under the same load. Report any movement too small to resolve with the ruler.
  2. Check the brace joins opposite joint centers and the room door still swings 90 degrees.

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: A braced test frame and four reinforced corners on the room model.
  2. Two diagonals and before/after racking data with a labeled frame sketch.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. A right-angled bay is 3 × 4 in. Calculate its diagonal. Identify which view would show a brace on a vertical wall.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
