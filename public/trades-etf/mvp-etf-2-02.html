P2 · 02 · Compare Flat, V and Channel Beams

Make three equal-paper beams and compare midpoint deflection over the same 8-in gap.

Name: __________________ Date: __________ · 110 minutes

Why this job?

Cross-section changes stiffness without necessarily changing material quantity. You will measure that effect before choosing structural shapes.

Daily kit · one crew

Each student: tape measure, pencil and graph notebook. Per crew: ruler, scissors and masking tape. Three 10 × 3-in paper strips, two equal-height book stacks, one small paper cup with five large paper clips, and a vertical ruler.

Finished means

  1. Three labeled beam shapes and one repeated sample.
  2. Notebook cross-sections and six test rows.

Brief · 10 minutes · Today’s exact job

  1. Why this job: Cross-section changes stiffness without necessarily changing material quantity. You will measure that effect before choosing structural shapes.
  2. Required today: Make three equal-paper beams and compare midpoint deflection over the same 8-in gap.
  3. Look at the supplied labeled drawing. Find the finished object or test setup before starting.
  4. Read the kit list. Take the listed continuing project out of storage; name missing items before beginning.

Notebook: Title/date; one sentence naming today’s exact object and its purpose.

Measure · 15 minutes · Measure and mark

  1. Set inside faces of the book stacks exactly 8 in apart. Mark the strip ends at 1 and 9 in.
  2. Measure the midpoint height relative to the tabletop, first empty and then loaded; use the same point on the beam.

Notebook: Feature | required value | actual value | units | datum/endpoints. Keep repeat readings.

Plan · 15 minutes · Read the plan and work the math

  1. Sketch the three end-on cross-sections and label equal blank area 10 × 3 = 30 in².
  2. Make table: shape | unloaded height mm | loaded height mm | deflection mm | collapsed?

Notebook: Use the named sketch/table in the directions. Label views, dimensions, units and revision.

Worked example · practice values

These supplied values teach the method. Build from today’s required plan dimensions; record your own actual test data.

  1. An 11-in beam is centered over an 8-in clear gap. Find the bearing length on each support.
  2. A centered 11-in member over an 8-in gap has (11 − 8)/2 = 1 1/2 in bearing at each end.
  3. The clear gap excludes the supports; total member length includes both bearings.

Build along · one move at a time

Maker acts. Checker compares with the plan. Swap at the pause. Use the complete numbered directions below.

Stop & show

Hold the three cross-sections side by side. Show two measured trials per shape.

Make · 45 minutes · Numbered bench directions

  1. Leave A flat. Fold B lengthwise at 1 1/2 in to make an open V. Fold C at 1 and 2 in to make a U-channel with a 1-in floor and upright sides.
  2. Place A with 1 in resting on each support. Mark its midpoint at 5 in from an end.
  3. Measure unloaded midpoint height. Place the same cup with five clips at the midpoint, release and measure after five seconds.
  4. Remove the load and repeat with B, ridge down so the cup bridges its upper edges, then C, channel opening up.
  5. Repeat the three tests once. If a beam collapses, record collapse instead of inventing a deflection.
  6. Make a second copy of the least-deflecting shape. Recheck its span and loading to see whether the result repeats.

Notebook: Label made parts and note any changed dimension. A short labeled sketch is enough; do not write an essay.

Test · 15 minutes · Test and inspect

  1. All blanks have equal area and use the same span, bearing, cup and clip load.
  2. Report two trials per shape. Do not call a collapsed specimen “zero deflection.”

Notebook: Copy the specified trial table; include each pass/fail or measured value and the change made.

Explain · 10 minutes · Show the finished work

  1. Put these on the bench: Three labeled beam shapes and one repeated sample.
  2. Notebook cross-sections and six test rows.
  3. Complete the independent transfer problem below. These supplied numbers are practice values; they do not change the required build dimensions.
  4. A beam spans 9 1/4 in with 3/4 in bearing at each end. What minimum length is needed? Sketch where the three lengths sit.
  5. Save one short individual answer with units, working and notebook page/date. Show the teacher your own physical measurement and plan interpretation. Store the labeled project and return shared supplies.

Notebook: Individual transfer calculation with units + page/date containing the practical evidence.
